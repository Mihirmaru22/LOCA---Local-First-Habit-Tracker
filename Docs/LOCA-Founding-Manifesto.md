# LOCA — Founding Manifesto

*The document that P1, P2, and P3 were reaching for and missed. It supersedes them.
No implementation. No APIs. No schema. Only the vision — and the refusals that protect
it.*

---

## The mistake we almost made

We spent three documents building an engine that **watches** a life. It reconstructed
the day, inferred hidden states, learned traits, and modeled a person with real rigor.
And it was pointed at the wrong target.

Every verb in that work was *watch, infer, understand, model*. The value accrued to the
**system's** picture of you. You were the thing being understood. We even priced your
own voice as "debt" — a cost the system pays to improve itself. That single word gave
the game away: a product that treats your participation as a liability has mistaken who
it is for.

Understanding a person is not the same as serving one. A perfectly accurate model of
your life that you cannot see and that does nothing for you is not a companion. It is
surveillance you invited into your own head.

## The truth we built everything else on

**You cannot see your own life.** You are inside it.

Your memory rewrites the past. You are too close to the present to read it. You cannot
predict how your own choices will feel. And you cannot hold the shape of your years in
your mind at once. A human being is *structurally unable to narrate their own story
while living it.*

This is not a flaw to fix in the user. It is the one thing software can genuinely give
that a person cannot give themselves: **perspective.**

## What LOCA is

LOCA is an **instrument** — the same kind of thing as a telescope or a compass, not the
same kind of thing as an app.

A telescope does not understand the stars. It does not tell you where to point your
life. It extends your sight, and what you do with what you see is entirely yours.

**LOCA is a telescope for your own life.** It gives you the vantage on yourself that
being alive denies you — and then it gets out of the way.

It is not a mirror (which only reflects, passively). It is not a coach (which prescribes,
with an agenda). It is a **second seat of perspective on your own life**, that remembers
what you forgot, notices what you're too close to see, holds the shape of your years,
and hands the living back to you.

## Our purpose

> **To give you the perspective on your own life that you cannot have from inside it —
> so you can meet your life with clarity, keep what mattered, and choose your next step
> on purpose instead of by default.**

Not to understand you. Not to improve you. To let you *see* — and leave the living to
you.

## The three refusals

These are load-bearing. Break any one and LOCA becomes something the world already has
too much of.

1. **Never a preacher.** LOCA holds no opinion about how you should live. It never tells
   you who to become, never carries a goal you didn't set, never optimizes a number on
   your behalf. It reflects, remembers, reveals. *You* decide. The moment it develops a
   view about your life, it has stopped being your instrument and started being someone
   else's.

2. **Never a second master.** LOCA is aligned with your clarity and nothing else. No
   ads. No engagement metric it's paid to grow. No behavior it profits from changing. No
   cloud that monetizes your life. It runs on your device because it answers to you
   alone. Every giant that could build this has a second master — hardware to lock in,
   attention to sell, inference to host. Having none is not a feature. It is the whole
   position.

3. **Never a black box.** LOCA shows its uncertainty instead of hiding it — "I might be
   wrong, but it looks like…" It holds its beliefs about you as *hypotheses you can see
   and correct*, never as verdicts. An instrument that blurs when it's blurry is honest.
   One that pronounces is to be feared.

## How a life should be represented

Forget the science for a moment. A life, seen so it can be *lived*, has a shape:

> **A self, moving through time, toward something, shaped by what it's made of and who
> it's with, leaving a trail.**

That shape is a **trajectory**. Its human-readable projection is a **story**. The two
are the same thing from two sides: the trajectory is the rigor, the story is the
interface. We model the trajectory and we speak in the story.

We choose this because it survives decades. The variables that define you *will* change
— at 25 the axes are ambition and love; at 45 they are meaning and health. A fixed list
of traits would be measuring the wrong things by then. **A trajectory doesn't break when
you become a different person. It just bends.** That is the only abstraction we have
found that is honest across a whole life.

## The ontology

Not a flat list of things to track — that is a database, and it has no subject. LOCA's
model has a subject and a direction. Six kinds of thing, arranged as an agent, not a
schema:

- **Moments** — the lived record. Gathered quietly, mostly on their own. The trail, not
  the model.
- **Patterns** — everything LOCA has learned about *how you work*, from a mood this
  afternoon to a lifelong rhythm. One kind of thing at every timescale — not sliced into
  separate boxes, because they are not separate: they are the same knowledge held with
  more or less stability.
- **People** — the relationships that shape you, each with its own history, closeness,
  and weather. First-class, because a person is not a place, and people are the largest
  cause of nearly everything else.
- **Places** — the settings and contexts a life moves through.
- **Chapters** — the meaningful eras, divided by the turning points that end one and
  begin the next. The shape of the trajectory, made legible. This is where a life reads
  as a life and not a log.
- **Direction** — what you value, what you're moving toward, who you're becoming. The
  first-person telos the old model had no place for. Without it, we were describing a
  marionette. With it, we describe the hand.

The self is **Direction and Patterns**. It moves through time as **Chapters**. It moves
among **People and Places**. It leaves **Moments** behind. That is the whole shape.

## How LOCA stays honest for decades

An instrument you cannot trust to be honest is worse than no instrument. Three
commitments keep it calibrated across a lifetime:

- **It learns most of the truth for free, and asks for the rest as a gift, not a tax.**
  Whatever the future will confirm — when you slept, what you did — LOCA predicts and
  then checks itself against what actually happened. No question needed. It only asks
  about what no sensor can reach: how something *felt*, what it *meant*. And because
  those questions serve *your* clarity — not the system's model — you *want* to answer
  them. A question in service of your own seeing is not a debt. It is a gift you give
  yourself.

- **It doubts itself where it matters.** LOCA does not assume it grows only more certain;
  a real life never settles, so neither does the model. It re-opens the beliefs whose
  wrongness would cost you most, and lets the trivial ones lie. When new evidence
  contradicts an old belief, the belief widens, it doesn't dig in.

- **It can re-narrate.** When LOCA has been wrong about you, it says so and tells a truer
  story — "I used to read your quiet evenings as retreat; the last months say they're
  rest." A model that can revise its story of you gets *stronger* from being wrong, not
  more brittle. Recovery from a false belief is not a failure mode. It is the mechanism.

## What "helping you live" actually means

LOCA's help is **your** agency, amplified by sight. Never ours, imposed.

**It does:** return what you forgot, at the moment it matters. Show you what you're too
close to see. Hold the shape of your years so you can feel where your life is heading.
And when *you* reach a fork, lay out what your own history has to say — then step back.

**It never does:** decide for you, want something for you that you didn't want first,
push a behavior, chase a metric, feign certainty, or speak when it has nothing true to
say.

The difference between LOCA and a coach is the difference between a telescope and a
preacher. One extends your sight. The other tells you what to do with it. We build the
telescope.

## Why we cannot be replaced

Anyone can build the features. Apple has more of your data than we ever will; the labs
have better models. None of that is the moat. Two things are, and they compound with
every year you use it:

- **A position the giants cannot take.** To be aligned with you and no one else — no ads,
  no cloud, no lock-in, no agenda — a company must have no second master. Every giant
  that could build this has one, and would have to cannibalize its own business to match
  us. The moat is standing in the one place their business models forbid them to stand.

- **A history they cannot copy.** Once LOCA has been the lens you saw your life through,
  and the second seat of perspective beside your decisions, leaving it is not switching
  apps — it is losing a part of how you think. A rival can offer a better instrument. No
  rival can offer you the life you already lived and understood through this one.

On your side, in your device, across your whole life. That is not a feature set. It is a
relationship, and it is the only durable thing.

## What we keep, and what we bury

**We keep:** reconstructing the day instead of summarizing it; gathering understanding
quietly from passive signal; treating turning points as structure, not noise; and the
absolute commitment to the user's device and the user's ownership.

**We bury:** the belief that the goal is for the *system* to understand the *user*. The
framing of the user's own voice as "debt." The ambition to disappear so completely the
app is forgotten — an instrument you never pick up is no instrument at all. And every
trace of an agenda for how a person ought to live.

## The test for every decision, forever

Before anything ships, one question — and it is not the old one.

The old test asked: *"Will this help LOCA understand the user better?"* That question
built an observer.

The new test asks:

> **"Will this help the user see their own life more clearly, and act more like
> themselves?"**

If it sharpens *your* sight and returns your life to *you*, it belongs. If it feeds a
model, drives a metric, or nudges a behavior, it does not — however clever it is.

We are not building a system that knows you. We are building the perspective that lets
you know yourself, and the quiet at the edge of it where you do the living.
