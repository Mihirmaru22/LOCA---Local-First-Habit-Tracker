# Phase R · R7 — Trust, Honesty, Transparency & Explainability (In Depth)

> **Mission:** learn **how products earn trust**, and encode the cardinal rule of a personal‑intelligence product: **it must never sound more certain than the evidence allows.**
>
> **Format:** every book and topic ends with the standard five blocks — **Lessons for LOCA · Anti‑patterns · Design implications · Open research questions · Ideas to experiment with** — tied to D1–D4.
>
> **The thesis R7 argues:** trust is **earned by calibration, provenance, and honest abstention — and lost, asymmetrically, by a single confident error.** LOCA's whole trust apparatus is *inverted*: it computes confidence and provenance (`UncertaintyCalculus`, `InferenceProvenance`) then **discards them (F8)**, renders a **deflated number as fact against a fabricated baseline (F1/F6)**, and shows **unexplained entities (F7)**. That is not a missing feature — it is the exact opposite of every trust mechanism below. R7 turns "earn trust" into a concrete, buildable **trust contract**.

> **Why this is existential for LOCA:** a life‑model product asks the user to believe things about *themselves*. The trust bar is therefore higher than for a search engine or a shopping app, and the failure mode — being confidently wrong about who someone is — is uniquely alienating (R2‑T10). Trust is not a polish layer here; it is the product's license to exist.

## Part A — How trust is earned (the framework)

Trust in a system decomposes (from the human‑trust‑in‑automation literature) into three components, each with a LOCA translation:

- **Ability/competence** — is it accurate? → LOCA's inference is *miscalibrated* (F1); competence is broken at the root.
- **Benevolence** — is it acting in my interest? → LOCA's local‑first stance is genuinely benevolent, but the overclaiming voice undermines the felt benevolence.
- **Integrity/honesty** — does it tell the truth, including about its limits? → LOCA *hides* its own uncertainty and provenance (F8); integrity is inverted.

On top of these sit five **trust behaviors** the best systems exhibit:

1. **Calibration** — confidence matches accuracy (Tetlock, Guo et al.).
2. **Provenance** — every claim shows its source (Apple Health, Perplexity).
3. **Abstention** — it says "I don't know" when it doesn't (selective prediction).
4. **Correctability** — the user can fix it, and it visibly learns (R4 feedback).
5. **Repair** — after an error, it acknowledges and recovers (trust‑repair research).

**The cardinal rule, operationalized:** *no claim renders unless it can show (a) its evidence, (b) a calibrated confidence, and (c) that it's within the data's support — otherwise the system abstains.* This single rule, enforced, fixes F1/F6/F7/F8 simultaneously.

## Part B — Book notes

### 1. *The Alignment Problem* — Brian Christian *(trust angle)*
A system earns trust only when its **objective matches the user's intent**; misaligned optimization (optimizing a proxy) produces confident, wrong behavior that erodes trust; interpretability and human feedback are the alignment (and trust) tools.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** LOCA is implicitly optimizing "**emit a reading**" (coverage) over "**be right**" (accuracy) — a small alignment failure that manufactures trust‑destroying false readings (F1). Realign the objective to *honesty*, and make it interpretable and correctable.
- **Anti‑patterns:** optimizing output coverage over correctness; opaque objectives; no feedback channel.
- **Design implications:** align the system goal with the user's interest (honest reflection); make reasoning inspectable; wire correction.
- **Open research questions:** what is LOCA actually optimizing, and does it match user intent? (Currently no.)
- **Ideas to experiment with:** re‑specify the inference objective to reward *appropriate abstention*, not coverage.

### 2. *The Book of Why* — Judea Pearl *(trust angle)*
Trust collapses when a system claims **causation it cannot support**; observational data yields only association (rung 1). Confident causal language ("X improved your Y") is both wrong and trust‑destroying.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** never cross from correlation to cause; hedged associational language *builds* trust by being honest about what the data can and can't show; surface confounders.
- **Anti‑patterns:** causal claims from correlation; hidden confounders; implied interventions.
- **Design implications:** strictly associational language; "these move together — does that fit you?"; confounder honesty.
- **Open research questions:** does associational hedging measurably increase trust vs confident causal claims?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** A/B causal vs associational phrasing of the same pattern; measure trust + accuracy of user belief.

### 3. *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments* — Ron Kohavi *(trust via decisions)*
Trust is also earned in **how you decide what to ship**: rigorous A/B testing, **guardrail metrics** (don't let one metric's gain silently harm another), avoiding twyman's‑law traps, and not shipping on intuition. Trustworthy *products* come from trustworthy *decisions*.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** validate every **trust‑affecting** change with experiments, and use **guardrail metrics** so engagement optimization can never silently degrade trust/honesty. (Ties R3's anti‑manipulation to a measurement discipline.)
- **Anti‑patterns:** shipping trust‑sensitive changes on intuition; optimizing engagement without a trust guardrail; p‑hacking a "win."
- **Design implications:** trust/honesty as a **guardrail metric** on every experiment; controlled tests for confidence/provenance changes.
- **Open research questions:** what measurable proxy for "trust" can serve as LOCA's guardrail metric?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** a trust guardrail (e.g. correction rate, "was this right?" yes‑rate) gating any Life‑surface change.

### 4. *Thinking, Fast and Slow* — Daniel Kahneman *(confidence & overconfidence)*
**Systematic overconfidence**: the illusion of validity, **WYSIATI** ("what you see is all there is" — we build confident stories from whatever data is present, ignoring what's missing), the planning fallacy. Confidence is a *feeling*, not evidence.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** LOCA's confident prose is **WYSIATI institutionalized** — it builds a confident reading from the little data present (F1: two weak signals → a confident "below baseline"), ignoring how much is missing. Confidence must come from *coverage and calibration*, not from the story sounding coherent.
- **Anti‑patterns:** confident narratives from thin data (WYSIATI); treating the system's "confidence" as evidence; ignoring missing data.
- **Design implications:** confidence gated on data coverage; explicitly represent "what's missing"; never let coherence masquerade as certainty.
- **Open research questions:** can LOCA quantify "how much is missing" and gate confidence on it?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** a coverage meter ("based on 2 of 6 possible signals") shown with every reading.

### 5. *Superforecasting* — Philip Tetlock *(the calibration gold standard)*
The best forecasters are **humble, probabilistic, granular, and self‑tracking**: they use calibrated probabilities, update frequently on new evidence, break problems down, and **score themselves (Brier scores)**. Calibration, not confidence, is the mark of expertise.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** adopt the superforecaster stance — **calibrated, granular, updated, self‑scored.** Use micro‑check‑ins as ground truth to **score and calibrate** LOCA's readings (a personal Brier score), and express confidence in calibrated terms.
- **Anti‑patterns:** binary/overconfident claims; never checking predictions against reality; uncalibrated confidence words.
- **Design implications:** calibrated probabilistic language; frequent updating; an internal calibration score from check‑ins.
- **Open research questions:** how fast can per‑user calibration converge from check‑ins? What granularity of confidence do users understand?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** an internal Brier‑style calibration tracker comparing readings to check‑ins; surface only well‑calibrated confidence.

### 6. *Noise* — Kahneman, Sibony & Sunstein
**Noise** = unwanted *variability* in judgment (two reads of the same situation differ), distinct from **bias** (systematic error). Both destroy accuracy; **decision hygiene** (aggregation, structure, independent inputs) reduces noise.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** LOCA has both — **bias** (F1's systematic deflation) *and* **noise** (jumpy hour‑to‑hour inference from sparse signals). Noisy, inconsistent readings erode trust as much as biased ones ("it said X yesterday, Y today, nothing changed"). Apply decision hygiene: **aggregate, smooth, and stabilize.**
- **Anti‑patterns:** jumpy per‑sample readings; inconsistent claims about the same period; presenting noise as signal.
- **Design implications:** smoothing/aggregation (EMA, R4); stability thresholds before changing a reading; distinguish noise from real change.
- **Open research questions:** how much smoothing removes noise without hiding real change? What inconsistency level breaks user trust?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** stability‑gated readings that only update when the change exceeds the noise floor.

## Part C — Research topics

### Group 1 — Trust itself

**T1. Human trust in AI.** Trust = f(ability, benevolence, integrity); shaped by first impressions, consistency, and error experience; over‑ and under‑trust both harmful.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* build all three; LOCA's benevolence (local‑first) is real but undercut by broken ability (F1) and integrity (F8).
- *Anti‑patterns:* competent‑seeming but wrong; hiding limits.
- *Design implications:* demonstrate accuracy, act in interest, disclose limits.
- *Open questions:* which component is LOCA's weakest lever?
- *Experiment:* measure trust before/after fixing calibration + provenance.

**T2. Appropriate trust calibration.** Match user trust to actual trustworthiness; avoid over‑reliance and dismissal (HAI, R4‑T2).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* signal confidence so users trust readings *exactly as much as warranted* — don't induce over‑trust with confident prose (F1).
- *Anti‑patterns:* uniform confidence; over‑trust induction.
- *Design implications:* confidence cues calibrate reliance.
- *Open questions:* which cues produce appropriate reliance?
- *Experiment:* confidence‑chip presence vs absence on reliance accuracy.

**T3. Human‑AI collaboration.** Complementary strengths; human retains judgment; AI proposes, human disposes.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* LOCA proposes reflections/observations; the **user is the final authority** (R2). Design for collaboration, not verdict.
- *Anti‑patterns:* AI‑as‑authority; removing human judgment.
- *Design implications:* proposal + user confirmation everywhere.
- *Open questions:* what division of labor feels most trustworthy?
- *Experiment:* verdict vs proposal framing.

**T4. Human oversight.** Meaningful human control; the user can inspect, override, and stop.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* user can inspect evidence, override readings, correct entities, and turn things off.
- *Anti‑patterns:* no override; opaque automation.
- *Design implications:* inspect/override/disable on every surface.
- *Open questions:* what oversight controls do users actually use?
- *Experiment:* add override to readings; measure use + trust.

**T5. AI accountability.** Who's responsible; auditability; a claim can be traced and questioned.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* every claim is auditable back to its events (Kleppmann log, R4); the system can always answer "why did you say that?"
- *Anti‑patterns:* unauditable claims; no trace.
- *Design implications:* full claim→evidence auditability.
- *Open questions:* what audit depth do users want?
- *Experiment:* a "trace this claim" affordance.

### Group 2 — Confidence & uncertainty

**T6. Confidence estimation.** Deriving honest confidence from data density + agreement, not model output.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* confidence from n + source agreement (LOCA has both). → **D3**.
- *Anti‑patterns:* raw‑output confidence; overconfidence on thin data.
- *Design implications:* density/agreement‑based confidence.
- *Open questions:* best confidence formula for LOCA's signals?
- *Experiment:* confidence = f(n, agreement) vs current.

**T7. Confidence communication.** How to express confidence — words, numbers, visual (bands/opacity); people interpret verbal probabilities *very* inconsistently ("likely" spans 30–90% across people).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* choose confidence encodings carefully; verbal hedges alone are ambiguous — pair with a visual/numeric anchor.
- *Anti‑patterns:* ambiguous verbal‑only hedges; false precision.
- *Design implications:* consistent, tested confidence encoding (word + visual).
- *Open questions:* which encoding is most consistently understood?
- *Experiment:* word vs band vs opacity comprehension test (also R5‑T31).

**T8. AI uncertainty estimation.** Quantify what's unknown; propagate it.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* `UncertaintyCalculus` already propagates uncertainty — **surface it**. → **D3**.
- *Anti‑patterns:* dropping uncertainty at the UI (F8).
- *Design implications:* uncertainty visible on every reading.
- *Open questions:* how to show propagated uncertainty simply?
- *Experiment:* surface existing uncertainty as a range.

**T9. Epistemic vs aleatoric uncertainty.** Reducible (more data) vs irreducible (noise).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* the sensor‑poor user's uncertainty is **epistemic** — "not enough yet," reducible — not a low value (F1). Communicate the difference.
- *Anti‑patterns:* collapsing epistemic into a point/low value.
- *Design implications:* "still learning" (epistemic) vs "inherently variable" (aleatoric) framings.
- *Open questions:* do users grasp the distinction if shown?
- *Experiment:* "still learning" state vs a number, on trust.

**T10. Calibration of ML models.** Reliability diagrams, ECE, temperature scaling; a calibrated 70% is right ~70% of the time (Guo et al.).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* calibrate readings against check‑in ground truth; report calibrated confidence. → **D4**.
- *Anti‑patterns:* uncalibrated confidence; never checking.
- *Design implications:* calibration loop from check‑ins.
- *Open questions:* convergence speed of per‑user calibration?
- *Experiment:* reliability diagram from check‑ins vs readings.

**T11. Human perception of confidence.** People misread confidence cues; overweight confident sources; anchor on the first number.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* since users over‑trust confident cues, **err toward under‑claiming**; design cues that resist over‑interpretation.
- *Anti‑patterns:* confident cues that invite over‑trust.
- *Design implications:* conservative confidence display; under‑claim bias.
- *Open questions:* what cue minimizes over‑interpretation?
- *Experiment:* conservative vs neutral confidence cues.

### Group 3 — Abstention & hallucination *(the cardinal‑rule core)*

**T12. Selective prediction.** Predict only when confident; abstain otherwise (predict‑or‑abstain with a coverage/accuracy tradeoff).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* gate every Life claim on a confidence threshold; below it, **abstain** ("not enough yet"). Directly enforces the cardinal rule; fixes F1's forced readings. → **D3/D4**.
- *Anti‑patterns:* always predicting (coverage over correctness); no abstention path.
- *Design implications:* a confidence gate with graceful abstention on every claim.
- *Open questions:* what coverage/accuracy tradeoff do users prefer?
- *Experiment:* selective prediction with an abstain state vs always‑predict.

**T13. Abstention ("I don't know").** The value — and difficulty — of declining to answer; users trust systems that admit ignorance.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* make "I don't have enough to say" a **first‑class, non‑failure output** (Christian, R4). Honest ignorance builds trust.
- *Anti‑patterns:* fabricating to fill a surface (F1); treating abstention as failure.
- *Design implications:* designed, dignified abstention states.
- *Open questions:* does abstention raise or lower perceived value?
- *Experiment:* abstain vs low‑confidence guess, on trust + satisfaction.

**T14. AI hallucinations.** Confident fabrication; the archetypal trust‑killer; grounding/RAG mitigates (R4).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* LOCA's *deterministic* analog to hallucination is **inventing a reading from insufficient data (F1) and fabricating a baseline (F6)** — same trust damage, no LLM required. Ground every claim in real events.
- *Anti‑patterns:* fabricated readings/baselines/entities (F1/F6/F7).
- *Design implications:* grounding + abstention forbid fabrication.
- *Open questions:* what's LOCA's fabrication rate on real (non‑seeded) data?
- *Experiment:* audit Life claims for grounding on a real cold‑start account.

### Group 4 — Explainability, provenance, evidence

**T15. Explainable AI (XAI).** Faithful explanations build trust; plausible‑but‑false ones erode it (R4‑T1).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* show the *real* contributors, not a plausible story. → **D3**.
- *Anti‑patterns:* unfaithful explanations.
- *Design implications:* faithful "why this reading."
- *Open questions:* explanation depth vs clutter?
- *Experiment:* expandable faithful "why."

**T16. AI transparency.** Openness about how it works, its data, and its limits.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* disclose the model, the data sources, and the limits plainly; transparency is benevolence made visible.
- *Anti‑patterns:* black‑box; hidden limits.
- *Design implications:* plain "how this works / what it can't do."
- *Open questions:* how much transparency helps vs overwhelms?
- *Experiment:* a concise "how LOCA works" surface.

**T17. Honest AI interfaces.** Interfaces engineered not to overclaim; language and visuals matched to evidence.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* every string/visual matched to the underlying confidence — an **honesty linter** for Life copy (R2‑Seligman tie‑in).
- *Anti‑patterns:* copy that overclaims relative to data.
- *Design implications:* confidence‑matched language + visuals.
- *Open questions:* can an automated honesty‑linter catch overclaims?
- *Experiment:* a copy linter flagging certainty > evidence.

**T18. Evidence visualization.** Showing the data behind a claim (the receipts).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* tap‑through from a claim to the exact `SignalEvent`s/logs behind it (R4/Kleppmann). → **D3**.
- *Anti‑patterns:* claims with no visible evidence.
- *Design implications:* claim → evidence drill‑down.
- *Open questions:* what evidence view is most convincing yet simple?
- *Experiment:* "show the events behind this" view.

**T19. Source attribution.** Citations; every derived fact names its origin (Apple Health, Perplexity).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* origin tag on every entity/claim (user/inferred/calendar/seeded) — the Sarah/Alex fix. → **D2/F7**.
- *Anti‑patterns:* unattributed data (F7).
- *Design implications:* mandatory origin badges.
- *Open questions:* badge granularity?
- *Experiment:* origin badges on People + readings.

**T20. Counterfactual explanations.** "If X had been different, the reading would change" — intuitive, actionable explanations.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* offer honest counterfactuals ("with sleep data, this read would be more confident") — explains *and* motivates permissions honestly.
- *Anti‑patterns:* counterfactuals implying causation (Pearl caution).
- *Design implications:* associational counterfactuals about *evidence*, not life causes.
- *Open questions:* which counterfactuals aid understanding without overclaiming?
- *Experiment:* an evidence‑counterfactual ("add Health for a sharper read").

**T21. Causal explanations.** Explaining via cause — powerful but dangerous; must be earned (Pearl).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* avoid causal explanations of the user's life; reserve for user‑run n=1 experiments only (R4).
- *Anti‑patterns:* causal life explanations from observation.
- *Design implications:* no causal claims outside earned n=1.
- *Open questions:* can LOCA ever earn a causal explanation safely?
- *Experiment:* n=1 opt‑in only.

### Group 5 — Trust repair, ethics, domain, consumer patterns

**T22. Trust repair after AI mistakes.** After an error: acknowledge, explain, correct; trust is **asymmetric** (hard to build, easy to lose) but *can* recover with visible correction.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* design the **error‑recovery** flow — when a user corrects LOCA, acknowledge gracefully and visibly update ("thanks — I'll hold that lightly / updated"). A good repair can *rebuild* trust.
- *Anti‑patterns:* ignoring corrections; defensive/no acknowledgment; silent (non‑updating) correction.
- *Design implications:* graceful, visibly‑updating correction responses.
- *Open questions:* can a good repair fully restore trust after a confident error?
- *Experiment:* correction flows with vs without acknowledgment/visible update.

**T23. AI accountability & responsible AI.** Governance, fairness, safety, documentation; the system is answerable for its claims.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* hold LOCA to a responsible‑AI bar — no harmful/false self‑claims (F1's false negativity is a wellbeing‑safety issue, R2‑Seligman).
- *Anti‑patterns:* unsafe self‑claims; no safety review.
- *Design implications:* a wellbeing‑safety review of Life copy.
- *Open questions:* what claims could harm a vulnerable user?
- *Experiment:* a safety audit of negative‑reading copy.

**T24. Personal AI ethics.** Modeling a *person* raises consent, autonomy, and dignity concerns beyond generic AI.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* model the user with consent, keep them the authority, preserve dignity (no reductive labels — R2).
- *Anti‑patterns:* non‑consensual modeling; reductive labeling; dignity loss.
- *Design implications:* consented, editable, dignified user model.
- *Open questions:* what modeling requires explicit consent?
- *Experiment:* a consented, user‑visible "what LOCA models about you."

**T25. Privacy‑preserving AI.** On‑device, differential privacy, federated learning; keep personal data local.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* LOCA's on‑device/local‑first stance is a core trust asset — foreground it (R4/R6). → benevolence made concrete.
- *Anti‑patterns:* off‑device personal data; cloud dependence.
- *Design implications:* on‑device by default; privacy promise foregrounded.
- *Open questions:* can all trust‑relevant compute stay on‑device?
- *Experiment:* an explicit "stays on your device" trust surface.

**T26. Responsible AI.** (Governance umbrella — see T23.) Fairness/safety/transparency as product requirements, not afterthoughts.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* bake transparency/safety into the definition of done for Life features.
- *Anti‑patterns:* trust as an afterthought.
- *Design implications:* trust in the DoD checklist.
- *Open questions:* what's the minimal responsible‑AI checklist for LOCA?
- *Experiment:* a per‑feature trust/safety checklist.

**T27. Transparency patterns in consumer AI products.** How shipping products show AI‑ness, confidence, and sources (Perplexity citations, "AI‑generated" labels, confidence chips, "why am I seeing this").
- *Lessons for LOCA:* adopt proven consumer patterns — citations, confidence chips, "why this," AI labels — rather than inventing.
- *Anti‑patterns:* hidden AI; no sources; no "why."
- *Design implications:* citation + confidence + "why" patterns.
- *Open questions:* which patterns fit a reflective (non‑chat) product?
- *Experiment:* adapt citation/confidence patterns to Life readings.

**T28. Trust in health & wellbeing AI.** Higher stakes; users are vulnerable; overclaiming can harm; must avoid implying clinical authority; caution and humility are mandatory.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* LOCA is wellbeing‑adjacent — **do not imply clinical/medical authority**, never diagnose, and treat false negative readings as potential harm (F1). Humble, non‑clinical framing throughout.
- *Anti‑patterns:* clinical‑sounding claims; diagnosis; confident negative wellbeing verdicts.
- *Design implications:* explicitly non‑clinical, humble wellbeing framing; safety rails on negative copy.
- *Open questions:* where is the line between reflection and (unlicensed) advice?
- *Experiment:* a non‑clinical language review of all wellbeing copy.

**T29. Why users stop trusting AI assistants.** Convergent causes: **confident errors**, **opacity**, **inconsistency** (noise), **creepiness** (over‑reach/surveillance feel), and **broken promises** (privacy/behavior).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* LOCA is exposed on **all five** — confident errors (F1), opacity (F8), inconsistency (noise), and creepiness (unexplained people, F7). The antidotes are R7's whole framework.
- *Anti‑patterns:* each of the five.
- *Design implications:* calibration (errors), provenance (opacity), smoothing (inconsistency), transparency+consent (creepiness), promise‑keeping (privacy).
- *Open questions:* which cause would churn LOCA users first?
- *Experiment:* a trust‑failure exit survey on a real cold‑start cohort.

**T30. Explainability UX.** (Interface half — see R5‑T26/R4.) Showing why/confidence/evidence in the interface.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* the UI must render the epistemics (why + confidence + evidence) — the visible form of everything above. → **D3**.
- *Anti‑patterns:* epistemics computed but not shown (F8).
- *Design implications:* why/confidence/evidence on screen.
- *Open questions:* minimal explainability‑UI that meaningfully raises trust?
- *Experiment:* add why+confidence+evidence to one reading; measure trust delta.

## Part D — R7 synthesis: the LOCA Trust Contract

### The answer: how products earn trust
By being **calibrated, transparent, provenanced, abstaining, correctable, and repair‑capable** — and by **never sounding more certain than the evidence allows.** Concretely, seven commitments:
1. **Calibrated confidence** matched to accuracy (Tetlock, calibration research).
2. **Provenance on every claim** (Apple Health, source attribution).
3. **Abstention** when data is thin — "not enough yet" is a valid, dignified output (selective prediction).
4. **No causal overreach** — association only (Pearl).
5. **Correctability** — the user teaches; it visibly updates (feedback loops).
6. **Repair** — errors are acknowledged and recovered gracefully (trust‑repair).
7. **Privacy kept** — on‑device promise foregrounded and honored.

### The Trust Contract (buildable spec for V3)
> **No Life claim renders unless it can show (a) its origin, (b) a calibrated confidence, and (c) that it's within the data's support. Below the confidence threshold, the system abstains ("still learning"). Every claim is correctable in one tap and, once corrected, visibly updates. No claim is causal. Nothing sounds more certain than its evidence.**

Enforcing this **one contract** resolves the entire D2/D3 cluster (F1 forced readings, F6 fabricated baseline, F7 unexplained entities, F8 discarded epistemics) at a stroke.

### Recurring trust principles
- **Calibration > confidence** (Tetlock).
- **Provenance by construction** (Apple Health, Kleppmann).
- **Abstention is a feature** (selective prediction).
- **Under‑claim** — trust is asymmetric; one confident error can end it (R2‑T10).
- **Reduce noise, not just bias** (Noise) — inconsistency erodes trust too.
- **The user is the authority and the corrector** (human‑AI collaboration).
- **Repair is possible** — design the recovery, not just the happy path.
- **Higher bar for wellbeing/personal AI** — humility is mandatory, not optional.

### Anti‑patterns (trust, additive to R2–R6)
- Confident output over thin data (F1) / WYSIATI narratives.
- Discarded confidence/provenance (F8); black‑box claims.
- Fabricated baselines/readings/entities (F1/F6/F7) — deterministic "hallucination."
- Causal claims from correlation.
- Jumpy, inconsistent readings (noise).
- No abstention path; coverage over correctness.
- No correction, or silent (non‑updating) correction.
- Clinical‑sounding or diagnostic wellbeing claims.
- Surveillance/creepiness (unexplained modeling of the person).

### Design implications for LOCA V3 (the trust program)
1. **Enforce the Trust Contract** as a hard gate on every Life claim. *(fixes F1/F6/F7/F8)*
2. **Surface calibrated confidence + range** on every reading; abstain below threshold. *(D3/D4)*
3. **Origin badge + one‑tap correction (visibly updating)** on every claim/entity. *(D2/D3)*
4. **Tap‑through to real evidence** for any claim. *(D3)*
5. **Smooth/stabilize inference** to cut noise; only update on real change. *(Noise)*
6. **Calibrate against check‑ins**; track an internal calibration score. *(Tetlock)*
7. **Non‑clinical, humble wellbeing language**; safety‑review negative copy. *(health‑AI trust)*
8. **Foreground the on‑device privacy promise.** *(benevolence)*
9. **Ship trust changes behind experiments** with a trust guardrail metric. *(Kohavi)*

### How R7 changes LOCA's direction
R4 said "add honesty, not intelligence." **R7 makes honesty *enforceable*: a single Trust Contract that gates every claim on evidence, confidence, and support — with abstention, provenance, correction, and repair built in.** LOCA's trust apparatus isn't missing; it's inverted. Turning it right‑side up — surfacing the epistemics it already computes and gating every claim on the cardinal rule — is simultaneously the fix for D2, D3, and the user‑facing symptoms of D4. Trust is not the last feature; it is the **license to make any claim at all.**
