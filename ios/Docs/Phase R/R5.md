# Phase R · R5 — Design, UX, Interaction & Product Experience (In Depth)

> **Mission:** answer the design question that decides whether LOCA's Life tab is usable — **how do you show complicated information without overwhelming the user?** — and turn the R1–R4 conclusions into concrete interface principles.
>
> **Format:** every book and topic ends with the standard five blocks — **Lessons for LOCA · Anti‑patterns · Design implications · Open research questions · Ideas to experiment with** — tied to D1–D4.
>
> **The thesis R5 argues:** the answer to "complex info without overwhelm" is a stack of four moves — **(1) one legible artifact first, (2) progressive disclosure to evidence, (3) calm/attention‑respecting delivery, (4) trust shown in the interface itself** (provenance, confidence, correction). LOCA violates all four today: it leads with a **prose wall + a 12‑card Browse row** (overwhelm, no hierarchy, no decision — D1) and **hides the provenance/confidence it computes** (no trust surface — D3). R5 is where the abstract findings become pixels.

> **What LOCA already gets right (preserve):** its **empty states** (`LifeReadEmptyCard`, `LifeEmptyState`) are genuinely good — honest, instructive, non‑punishing. R5's job is to raise the rest of the UI to that bar, not to touch what works.

---

## Part A — The central question, answered as a design framework

**"Show complexity without overwhelm" = a disclosure stack:**

1. **Summary object first.** One glanceable, decision‑ or reflection‑relevant artifact (Oura's Readiness; a single reflective prompt). Never lead with prose or a grid.
2. **Progressive disclosure.** Detail on demand — tap the artifact to reach contributors, then evidence, then raw events (Kleppmann's log, R4). Depth exists but is *optional*.
3. **Calm delivery.** Respect attention and working‑memory limits (Miller, cognitive load); interrupt only when it repays the interruption (calm tech).
4. **Trust in the interface.** Provenance badges, confidence cues, and correction affordances are *visual elements*, not hidden metadata (Apple Health's source labels; explainability UX).

Every book and topic below is a lens on one of these four moves. LOCA's redesign is, concretely, **collapse the prose+grid into a summary object, make everything drill to evidence, calm the delivery, and put trust on screen.**

## Part B — Book notes

### 1. *Don't Make Me Think* — Steve Krug
Usability's first law: **self‑evidence.** Users **scan, satisfice, and muddle through**; they don't read, they don't puzzle out. Every question the interface forces ("what is this? what do I do here?") is friction. Conventions exist because they remove thought.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** the Life tab currently *makes users think* — a prose paragraph to parse and 12 undifferentiated cards to evaluate. Replace with a self‑evident summary object and an obvious next action.
- **Anti‑patterns:** prose that must be read to extract a point; ambiguous cards; novel navigation that breaks conventions.
- **Design implications:** scannable hierarchy; obvious primary action; conventional patterns; one clear "what this is."
- **Open research questions:** what's the single most self‑evident daily Life artifact for LOCA?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** usability‑test the current Life tab with Krug's "what is this page / what can I do" questions.

### 2. *The Design of Everyday Things* — Don Norman
**Affordances, signifiers, mapping, feedback, conceptual models, constraints, error tolerance.** Good design makes the right action discoverable and errors recoverable; a clear **conceptual model** lets users predict behavior.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** users need a clear conceptual model of *what LOCA is* (a mirror/record, not an oracle). Actions (reflect, correct, drill to evidence) must be discoverable via signifiers; lapses must be recoverable (R3 error tolerance).
- **Anti‑patterns:** hidden affordances (the buried micro‑check‑in, pre‑fix); no feedback on actions; punishing errors; a confused conceptual model ("does it know me or guess?").
- **Design implications:** clear signifiers for reflect/correct/evidence; immediate feedback; a stated conceptual model; recoverable everything.
- **Open research questions:** what conceptual model do users form of LOCA today, and is it the honest one?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** a one‑line "what LOCA is" model statement in onboarding, tested for comprehension.

### 3. *About Face* — Alan Cooper
Goal‑directed interaction design; **what users do ≠ what they say**; eliminate **excise** (work that serves the system, not the goal); design for the goal, not the feature.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** design for the user's *goal* (understand/reflect on my life), not the backend's features (12 model outputs as 12 cards — that's excise). Cut interface work that serves the model, not the user.
- **Anti‑patterns:** feature‑mirroring UI (one card per engine output); excise; designing to what users *say* they want (more data) vs what serves the goal (reflection).
- **Design implications:** goal‑directed IA; hide the backend's structure; remove model‑serving excise.
- **Open research questions:** which current Life surfaces are excise (serve the model) vs value (serve the goal)?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** audit every Life surface: "whose goal — model's or user's?"

### 4. *Refactoring UI* — Adam Wathan & Steve Schoger
Practical visual craft: **hierarchy via size/weight/color**, generous spacing, **design in grayscale first** (hierarchy before color), **emphasize by de‑emphasizing**, limit choices, avoid gray text on colored backgrounds, use real content.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** LOCA's Life tab lacks visual hierarchy — prose and equal‑weight cards give the eye nothing to prioritize. Establish hierarchy (one dominant element), de‑emphasize the rest, add breathing room.
- **Anti‑patterns:** equal‑weight elements (the 12 identical cards); dense, cramped layout; color as the only hierarchy device.
- **Design implications:** one dominant summary element; de‑emphasized secondary surfaces; grayscale‑first hierarchy; generous spacing.
- **Open research questions:** what's the right visual weight ratio between the daily artifact and Browse?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** redesign the Life tab in grayscale to force real hierarchy before adding color.

### 5. *Laws of UX* — Jon Yablonski
A compendium of design‑relevant psychology laws (Hick's, Fitts's, Jakob's, Miller's, Peak‑End, Aesthetic‑Usability, Von Restorff, Serial Position). A practical bridge from cognition (R2) to interface.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** apply the laws directly — reduce choices (Hick's: the 12 cards), chunk (Miller's), design the peak and end (Peak‑End), meet convention (Jakob's), and make the important thing distinct (Von Restorff).
- **Anti‑patterns:** violating each law — too many choices, no chunking, forgettable/negative endings, novel navigation.
- **Design implications:** a law‑by‑law checklist for the Life tab (see Part C topics T17–T20).
- **Open research questions:** which law is LOCA violating most costly?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** a "Laws of UX" audit of the Life tab.

### 6. *100 Things Every Designer Needs to Know About People* — Susan Weinschenk
Applied cognitive/behavioral science for design: perception, attention, memory limits, motivation, error — the empirical "why" behind UI patterns (peripheral vision, chunking, the power of stories, progress motivation).
- **Lessons for LOCA:** ground every UI choice in how people actually perceive/attend/remember — e.g. people are motivated by progress and stories (R2), remember peaks, and can't hold many items. Design to the mind's real constraints.
- **Anti‑patterns:** UI that ignores perceptual/memory limits; walls of text; no progress signals.
- **Design implications:** chunked, progress‑aware, story‑shaped surfaces within cognitive limits.
- **Open research questions:** which cognitive constraint most shapes LOCA's Life comprehension?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** apply Weinschenk's memory/attention findings to the daily artifact design.

### 7. *Seductive Interaction Design* — Stephen Anderson
Engagement and delight *ethically*: the **aesthetic‑usability effect** (attractive things feel more usable), motivation, playful moments — but in service of the user, echoing R3's manipulation caution.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** craft and small delight raise perceived usability and trust — worth investing in — but delight must be **honest** (never confetti for a fake win, R2/Heath). Aesthetic quality itself builds trust.
- **Anti‑patterns:** manipulative delight; engagement mechanics over substance (R3); dark‑pattern "seduction."
- **Design implications:** invest in polish and honest micro‑delight; keep it ethical and substance‑serving.
- **Open research questions:** does aesthetic polish measurably raise trust in LOCA's readings?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** a polished, honestly‑celebratory milestone moment (real data only).

### 8. *Microinteractions* — Dan Saffer
The small moments — **trigger, rules, feedback, loops/modes** — define product feel. A great microinteraction (a satisfying log confirmation, a gentle state transition) is where quality is felt.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** the check‑in confirmation, the reflection save, the drill‑to‑evidence tap are microinteractions worth crafting — feel is built here. LOCA's post‑log confirmation (T1 interpretation card) is one such moment.
- **Anti‑patterns:** janky/absent feedback; inconsistent microinteraction behavior; missed moments of feel.
- **Design implications:** deliberate feedback on every action; craft the key microinteractions (log, reflect, correct, drill).
- **Open research questions:** which microinteraction most shapes LOCA's felt quality?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** redesign the log→reflect handoff as a single crafted microinteraction.

### 9. *Universal Principles of Design* — William Lidwell
An encyclopedia of ~125 cross‑disciplinary principles (signal‑to‑noise, progressive disclosure, Hick's, Fitts's, Von Restorff, chunking, hierarchy, etc.) — a reference to check designs against.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** use as a checklist; the most relevant — **signal‑to‑noise** (cut noise from the Life tab), **progressive disclosure**, **hierarchy**, **highlighting** — all point the same way.
- **Anti‑patterns:** low signal‑to‑noise (prose + grid); flat hierarchy; no progressive disclosure.
- **Design implications:** maximize signal‑to‑noise; layer disclosure; enforce hierarchy.
- **Open research questions:** which principle, applied, most improves Life comprehension?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** a signal‑to‑noise pass on every Life surface.

### 10. *Designing Interfaces* — Jenifer Tidwell
A **pattern library** for interface problems (navigation, lists, forms, dashboards, disclosure). Reusable, tested solutions.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** reuse proven patterns rather than inventing (Jakob's Law) — for the daily artifact, disclosure, and trust surfaces there are established patterns to adopt.
- **Anti‑patterns:** novel patterns where conventions exist; reinventing navigation.
- **Design implications:** adopt established disclosure/dashboard/list patterns; deviate only with reason.
- **Open research questions:** which existing patterns best fit LOCA's reflect‑and‑drill model?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** map each Life surface to a known Tidwell pattern.

### 11. *Rocket Surgery Made Easy* — Steve Krug
**DIY usability testing**: cheap, frequent, few‑user tests reveal most problems; you don't need a lab. Test early, test often, fix the most serious thing first.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** *test the Life redesign with real users, cheaply and often.* Every R1–R5 hypothesis (does the artifact land? do users trust a hedged reading?) is a testable question, not a matter of opinion.
- **Anti‑patterns:** shipping the redesign untested; over‑researching instead of testing; ignoring the most serious finding.
- **Design implications:** lightweight recurring usability tests; fix‑the‑worst‑first discipline.
- **Open research questions:** which R1–R5 hypotheses are cheapest to falsify with 3–5 users?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** a 5‑user test of the current Life tab before redesigning, to anchor on real problems.

## Part C — Research topics

### Group 1 — Systems & standards

**T1. Apple HIG.** Clarity, deference (content over chrome), depth (layered navigation); platform conventions; Dynamic Type, SF Symbols, standard controls.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* defer to content (the record/reflection), use depth for progressive disclosure, follow iOS conventions (Jakob's). LOCA is a SwiftUI app — lean on the platform.
- *Anti‑patterns:* chrome over content; fighting platform conventions.
- *Design implications:* HIG‑native components, Dynamic Type, layered depth.
- *Open questions:* where does LOCA deviate from HIG without reason?
- *Experiment:* an HIG conformance pass.

**T2. Material Design.** Google's system — elevation, motion, responsive grid, tokens. Not LOCA's platform, but its **design‑token discipline** and motion guidance transfer.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* borrow token discipline (LOCA has a `DS` design system) and purposeful motion, not the visual language.
- *Anti‑patterns:* mixing platform idioms incoherently.
- *Design implications:* consistent tokens; purposeful motion.
- *Open questions:* is LOCA's `DS` system complete/consistent?
- *Experiment:* audit `DS` tokens for coverage/consistency.

**T3. NN/g usability research.** Nielsen's 10 heuristics; empirical usability method; discount usability testing.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* audit the Life tab against the 10 heuristics (visibility of status, match to real world, user control, consistency, error prevention, recognition, flexibility, aesthetic minimalism, error recovery, help).
- *Anti‑patterns:* violating heuristics (esp. #8 minimalism, #6 recognition, #10 evidence/help).
- *Design implications:* heuristic‑clean surfaces.
- *Open questions:* which heuristic does LOCA fail worst?
- *Experiment:* a 10‑heuristic evaluation.

### Group 2 — Structure & disclosure *(LOCA's D1 core)*

**T4. Progressive disclosure.** Show essentials; reveal detail on demand. The central technique for complexity‑without‑overwhelm.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* the spine of the redesign — summary → contributors → evidence → raw events. LOCA's Reach gesture already gestures at this.
- *Anti‑patterns:* everything at once (prose + 12 cards); no depth.
- *Design implications:* layered disclosure on every claim.
- *Open questions:* right number of disclosure layers?
- *Experiment:* a 3‑layer disclosure for the daily artifact.

**T5. Information architecture.** Organization, labeling, structure; findability.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* IA should reflect the user's mental model (reflect / browse my life / ask), not the backend's 12 engines.
- *Anti‑patterns:* backend‑mirroring IA; unclear labels.
- *Design implications:* goal‑based IA and labels.
- *Open questions:* what IA matches users' mental model of "my life"?
- *Experiment:* card‑sort the Life surfaces with users.

**T6. Information hierarchy.** What matters most, made most prominent.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* one dominant daily artifact; everything else subordinate. (Refactoring UI.)
- *Anti‑patterns:* flat hierarchy (equal cards).
- *Design implications:* explicit primary/secondary/tertiary.
- *Open questions:* what is the single most important Life element?
- *Experiment:* a strict three‑tier hierarchy.

**T7. Interaction design.** Behavior over time; flows, states, transitions.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* design the reflect/correct/drill *flows*, not just screens.
- *Anti‑patterns:* screen‑by‑screen thinking; dead‑end states.
- *Design implications:* flow‑level design; no dead ends.
- *Open questions:* what's the core daily interaction flow?
- *Experiment:* map the ideal daily flow end‑to‑end.

**T8. Visual hierarchy.** Guiding the eye via size, contrast, position, whitespace.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* make the eye land on the daily artifact first; de‑emphasize the rest.
- *Anti‑patterns:* competing focal points.
- *Design implications:* one focal point per view.
- *Open questions:* does the eye currently land anywhere meaningful?
- *Experiment:* a squint/blur test of the Life tab.

### Group 3 — Surfaces & moments

**T9. Dashboard design.** Summary‑first, glanceable, KPI‑led; detail beneath.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* if LOCA has a dashboard, it's summary‑first — but R1 warns *a dashboard is not a decision*; prefer a reflective artifact over a metrics dashboard.
- *Anti‑patterns:* metric‑dump dashboard mistaken for insight (Gyroscope, R1).

**T11. First‑use experience.** First value fast; ask little; postpone with payoff (R6 overlaps).

- *Anti‑patterns:* empty first run; permission walls; heavy setup.

**T12. Navigation patterns.** Tabs, hierarchy, modals; predictable movement.

**T13. Mobile UX best practices.** Thumb zones, ≥44pt touch targets, one‑hand use, performance.

### Group 4 — Accessibility & the cognitive laws

**T14. Accessibility (WCAG).** Contrast, semantics, Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, reduce‑motion, alternatives.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* full Dynamic Type/VoiceOver/contrast/reduce‑motion support; accessibility is non‑negotiable and improves design for all.
- *Anti‑patterns:* low contrast; unlabeled controls; motion without reduce‑motion.
- *Design implications:* WCAG‑AA minimum; VoiceOver labels; honor reduce‑motion.
- *Open questions:* current accessibility gaps?
- *Experiment:* a VoiceOver + Dynamic Type audit of the Life tab.

**T15. Cognitive load.** Intrinsic/extraneous/germane; minimize extraneous load.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* the prose+grid is high extraneous load; cut it. (R3/cognitive psych.)
- *Anti‑patterns:* extraneous load (decoration, density, choices).
- *Design implications:* strip extraneous load; one thing at a time.
- *Open questions:* where is LOCA's highest extraneous load?
- *Experiment:* remove one element per Life view and test comprehension.

**T16. Recognition vs recall.** Show options; don't make users remember. (Nielsen #6.)
- *Lessons for LOCA:* surface affordances (reflect/correct/drill) visibly; don't hide them (the buried micro‑check‑in was a recall failure).
- *Anti‑patterns:* hidden features requiring recall.
- *Design implications:* visible, recognizable affordances.
- *Open questions:* which LOCA actions currently require recall?
- *Experiment:* surface every hidden action.

**T17. Hick's Law.** Choice time grows with number/complexity of options.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* cut the 12‑card Browse to a prioritized few; fewer choices, faster action.
- *Anti‑patterns:* 12 equal choices.
- *Design implications:* reduce and prioritize choices.
- *Open questions:* minimal viable Browse set?
- *Experiment:* Browse with 3 prioritized entries vs 12.

**T18. Fitts's Law.** Target time ∝ distance/size; make primary targets big and near.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* large, close primary actions (reflect/log).
- *Anti‑patterns:* small/distant primary targets.
- *Design implications:* big thumb‑near primary buttons.
- *Open questions:* are primary targets optimally sized/placed?
- *Experiment:* enlarge/relocate the primary reflect action.

**T19. Miller's Law.** ~7±2 (really ~4) chunks in working memory; chunk information.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* chunk Life into ≤ a handful of groups; never present 12 flat items.
- *Anti‑patterns:* unchunked lists exceeding memory.
- *Design implications:* chunk into ≤4 groups.
- *Open questions:* right chunking of Life surfaces?
- *Experiment:* group the 12 cards into 3–4 categories.

**T20. Jakob's Law.** Users expect your app to work like the others they know.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* use conventional iOS patterns; don't invent novel interactions where a standard exists.
- *Anti‑patterns:* novel navigation/gestures with no payoff.
- *Design implications:* convention by default; novelty only with clear benefit (the Reach gesture must earn its novelty).
- *Open questions:* does the Reach gesture help or confuse vs a conventional pattern?
- *Experiment:* Reach gesture vs a conventional drill‑down, tested.

**T21. Peak‑End Rule.** Experiences are remembered by peak + end (Kahneman, R2).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* design the peak (a meaningful milestone/insight) and the end (session close, weekly review) deliberately; these shape memory of the whole product.
- *Anti‑patterns:* flat, endless, endless‑scroll experiences with no peak/end.
- *Design implications:* deliberate peak moments; satisfying session/period endings.
- *Open questions:* what's LOCA's designed peak and end?
- *Experiment:* a crafted end‑of‑week closing moment.

### Group 5 — Calm, motion, notifications

**T22. Calm Technology.** Inform from the periphery; demand minimal attention (Weiser/Brown, R3).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* the Life surface should reward a glance and demand nothing; interrupt only when it repays.
- *Anti‑patterns:* attention‑hungry UI/notifications returning little.
- *Design implications:* peripheral, glanceable, low‑demand.
- *Open questions:* what's the calmest useful daily surface?
- *Experiment:* a glance‑only Life summary (widget‑grade).

**T23. Notification UX.** Relevance, timing, control, batching; every notification is a withdrawal from trust.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* LOCA lacks a Life notification (the missing ritual, R1) — when added, make it **rare, relevant, controllable, batched** (calm tech + R3 anti‑manipulation).
- *Anti‑patterns:* frequent/low‑value/guilt notifications; no controls.
- *Design implications:* a single high‑value weekly ritual notification, user‑controlled.
- *Open questions:* what cadence sustains the ritual without fatigue (R3‑T19)?

**T24. Motion & animation principles.** Purposeful (orient, show causality, provide feedback); respect **reduce‑motion**; never gratuitous.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* motion to orient during disclosure transitions and confirm actions; honor reduce‑motion (LOCA already reads `reduceMotion` in places).
- *Anti‑patterns:* decorative/janky motion; ignoring reduce‑motion.

- *Open questions:* where does motion aid comprehension of disclosure?
- *Experiment:* orienting transitions between disclosure layers.

### Group 6 — Trust, uncertainty, reflection, personal analytics *(LOCA's D3 core)*

**T25. Data visualization for personal analytics.** Honest, glanceable, **baseline‑relative**; small multiples/sparklines; endpoint emphasis; never mislead with scale.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* viz must be baseline‑relative (Oura), honest about uncertainty (show ranges/thin‑data), and glanceable; never chart a deflated/placeholder value as fact (F1/F6).
- *Anti‑patterns:* misleading scales; charting thin‑data as confident; decoration over clarity.

- *Open questions:* which viz best conveys "state vs your baseline, with uncertainty"?

**T26. Explainability UX for AI.** Show **why** (real contributors), **confidence**, and **evidence**; make reasoning inspectable (R4/XAI).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* surface the `contributingSources`/`sampleCount` LOCA already computes; tap‑through to evidence. → **D3**.
- *Anti‑patterns:* black‑box claims; hidden reasoning (F8).
- *Design implications:* "why this reading" with real factors + confidence + evidence.
- *Open questions:* how much explanation before it's clutter?
- *Experiment:* an expandable "why" on every reading.

**T27. Trust‑centered interface design.** Transparency, provenance, user control, honest defaults, correction (Apple Health, R4).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* provenance badges, confidence cues, and a one‑tap correction are core UI, not metadata. → **D2/D3**.
- *Anti‑patterns:* unlabeled data (F7); no correction; opaque confidence.
- *Design implications:* origin badge + confidence + "that's wrong" on every claim/entity.
- *Open questions:* minimal trust‑UI set that meaningfully raises trust?
- *Experiment:* add origin badges + correction to People and readings; measure trust.

**T28. Reflection product design.** Low‑friction prompts, space to write, seeded (not blank) prompts, structure + distance (R2/R3).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* the primary Life loop — a seeded, self‑distanced, two‑line reflection anchored to a real moment.
- *Anti‑patterns:* blank prompts; heavy forms; immersive‑rumination framing.
- *Design implications:* seeded, structured, tiny reflection UI.
- *Open questions:* what prompt UI maximizes completion + quality?
- *Experiment:* a seeded two‑line reflection card as the Life home.

**T29. Journal app UX teardown — Day One, Apple Journal, Reflectly, Daylio, Stoic, Rosebud.**
- **Day One:** beautiful, media‑rich, but friction/guilt at the blank page → borrow the polish, avoid the friction.
- **Apple Journal:** suggestion‑seeded prompts, no claims → *the model UX* (R1); borrow wholesale.
- **Reflectly:** AI‑framed, prompt‑led journaling → borrow prompt‑leading, watch for gimmick.
- **Daylio:** ~5‑second structured entry → borrow radical low friction.
- **Stoic:** ritual + prompt library + mood → borrow ritual cadence.
- **Rosebud:** AI reflective journaling (LLM prompts that respond) → borrow responsive‑reflection *if* grounded and private (R4 caution); avoid ungrounded AI claims.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* synthesize the winners — Apple Journal's suggestion‑seeding + Daylio's friction + Stoic's ritual + (carefully) Rosebud's responsiveness, all over LOCA's real record.
- *Anti‑patterns:* Day One's blank‑page guilt; Rosebud‑style ungrounded AI claims about the user.
- *Design implications:* seeded, ~5‑second, ritualized, optionally‑responsive reflection, grounded + private.
- *Open questions:* does responsive (AI) reflection beat static prompts for LOCA users, without hallucination?
- *Experiment:* static seeded prompt vs grounded responsive prompt, A/B.

**T30. Personal dashboard design patterns.** Cards, summaries, trends, drill‑down; the pattern language of self‑analytics.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* use sparingly and honestly; a *reflective* home likely beats a dashboard (R1's dashboard warning), with dashboard patterns reserved for the honest habit rollups (This Week/Monthly).
- *Anti‑patterns:* dashboard‑as‑insight; metric overload.
- *Design implications:* reflective home + honest habit dashboards for the FACT surfaces.
- *Open questions:* which LOCA data deserves a dashboard vs a reflection?
- *Experiment:* keep dashboards for FACT rollups only; reflection for everything inferred.

**T31. Designing for uncertainty & confidence communication.** Show ranges not points; hedged language; "still learning" states; visual confidence encodings (opacity, width, bands).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* this is the interface half of R4 — every inferred reading shows **confidence and range**, not a false point; thin data → "still learning," never a deflated number (F1). → **D3/D4**.
- *Anti‑patterns:* false precision; point values on thin data; confident language over low confidence.
- *Design implications:* ranges + confidence cues + hedged copy + "still learning" everywhere inference appears.
- *Open questions:* which confidence encoding is most intuitively understood (band? opacity? word?)?
- *Experiment:* test range‑band vs confidence‑word vs opacity for the same reading.

## Part D — R5 synthesis

### The answer to the central question
**How do you show complicated information without overwhelming users?** Stack four moves, in order:
1. **One legible artifact first** (hierarchy, Refactoring UI/Miller/Von Restorff) — never prose or a grid.
2. **Progressive disclosure to evidence** (HIG depth, Universal Principles) — depth is optional.
3. **Calm, attention‑respecting delivery** (Calm Tech, cognitive load, notification UX).
4. **Trust shown in the interface** (provenance badges, confidence cues, correction — explainability/trust UX).

Applied to LOCA: **collapse the prose + 12‑card Browse into a single reflective artifact; make every claim drill to its real evidence; calm the delivery to a glance + one weekly ritual; and put provenance, confidence, and correction on screen.**

### Recurring design principles
- **Self‑evidence** (Krug): never make the user think about the interface.
- **Hierarchy & signal‑to‑noise** (Refactoring UI, Lidwell): one dominant thing; cut the rest.
- **Progressive disclosure** (HIG, Universal Principles): summary → evidence → raw.
- **Fewer choices, chunked** (Hick's, Miller's): kill the 12‑card flat grid.
- **Convention over novelty** (Jakob's, Tidwell): reuse patterns; the Reach gesture must earn its novelty.
- **Peak & end, designed** (Peak‑End): craft the milestone and the close.
- **Calm** (Weiser/Brown): reward a glance; interrupt rarely.
- **Trust on screen** (Apple Health, explainability/trust UX): provenance + confidence + correction are UI.
- **Accessible by default** (WCAG): Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, contrast, reduce‑motion.
- **Test it** (Krug's Rocket Surgery): falsify design hypotheses with 3–5 users.

### Anti‑patterns (interface, additive to R2–R4)
- Prose wall + flat 12‑card grid as the Life home (overwhelm, no hierarchy — D1).
- Discarded provenance/confidence; black‑box claims (F8 — D3).

### Design implications for LOCA V3 (the interface program)
1. **Redesign the Life home as one reflective artifact** (seeded two‑line reflection or a single baseline‑relative read), with strict three‑tier hierarchy. *(D1)*
2. **Every inferred claim renders confidence + range + tap‑through evidence**; thin data → "still learning," never a number. *(D3/D4)*
3. **Provenance badge + one‑tap correction on every claim/entity** (origin: user/inferred/calendar/seeded). *(D2/D3)*
4. **Collapse the 12‑card Browse** into ≤4 chunked groups or a prioritized list. *(Hick's/Miller's)*
5. **Preserve and extend the empty‑state pattern** to claim‑level "not enough yet." *(strength)*
6. **Add exactly one calm weekly ritual notification**, opt‑in, controllable. *(the missing return engine, done calmly)*
7. **Accessibility pass** (Dynamic Type/VoiceOver/contrast/reduce‑motion) across Life.
8. **Test every hypothesis** with 3–5 users before building broadly. *(Krug)*

### How R5 changes LOCA's direction
R1–R4 said *what* LOCA should become (a reflective, honest, private life‑record). **R5 says how it must *look and feel*: one legible artifact, progressive disclosure to real evidence, calm delivery, and trust rendered as visible interface — provenance, confidence, and correction you can see and touch.** The Life tab's current prose‑wall‑plus‑grid is not a content problem; it is a **design** problem, and it is solvable with well‑established principles LOCA is currently ignoring.
