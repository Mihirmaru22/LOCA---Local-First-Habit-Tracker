# Phase R · R8 — Personal Data, Sensors, Digital Health & Context (In Depth)

> **Mission:** determine **which personal data actually predicts meaningful life outcomes, and which is noise** — so LOCA collects and weights signal honestly instead of inferring a rich inner state from weak inputs.
>
> **Format:** every book and topic ends with the standard five blocks — **Lessons for LOCA · Anti‑patterns · Design implications · Open research questions · Ideas to experiment with** — tied to D1–D4.
>
> **The thesis R8 argues:** the digital‑phenotyping literature is clear — **passive sensor + behavior data can predict states and outcomes, but only modestly, noisily, individually, and with heavy missing‑data problems.** That is the empirical ground under **D4**: LOCA infers four emotional dimensions from **habit checkboxes** (a weak inner‑state signal) and, for the default sensor‑poor user, treats **missing data as low value (F1)** — a textbook missing‑data error. R8 ranks the signals, exposes LOCA's data mismatch, and names the highest‑value data LOCA is under‑using (explicit notes/check‑ins, sleep *regularity*, social/mobility).

> **LOCA's sensing architecture, from the code (real breadth):** `HealthKitManager` (sleep, HRV, HR, steps, workouts, mindful), `CalendarManager`, `LocationManager`, `MotionActivityManager`, `DaylightManager`, `DeviceActivityManager` (screen time), plus `HabitBridgeManager` — all gated by `SignalSourceConsent` (a real consent ledger) with `SourceProvenanceManager` tracking provenance. Architecturally this is a *good* multi‑source, consented, provenance‑aware sensing stack. The problems are downstream: **most users grant nothing → sensor‑poor → F1**, the signals are **weak inner‑state predictors**, and **missing data is mishandled**.

## Part A — The signal‑vs‑noise ranking (the empirical core)

From digital‑health and wellbeing research, ranked by evidence for predicting wellbeing/behavior **within a person over time** (the n=1 regime that matters for LOCA):

**High signal**
- **Sleep — especially *regularity/timing*** (regularity predicts mood/wellbeing better than duration alone).
- **Physical activity** (movement, workouts) — robust wellbeing correlate.
- **Social interaction** (quantity/quality) — one of the strongest wellbeing predictors.
- **Mobility patterns** (location variety/range) — reduced mobility ↔ low mood (digital‑phenotyping finding).
- **The user's own explicit mood/notes** — the most direct signal available.
- **Sunlight / light exposure** — circadian and mood relevance.

**Medium signal (individual, noisy — need personal baselines)**
- **Resting HR / HRV** (recovery/stress proxies; very noisy day‑to‑day; useless without a personal baseline).
- **Calendar density** (busyness/stress proxy; confounded).
- **Respiratory rate** (illness/stress; sleep‑derived).

**Low signal / noise‑alone**
- **Step‑count‑as‑mood**, **screen‑time‑as‑focus** (weak, ambiguous, context‑dependent).
- **Single‑day HRV** (dominated by noise).
- **Weather** (real but small effect; a confounder more than a driver).
- **Habit‑checkbox‑completion as an emotional‑state proxy** — a completed habit is strong evidence *the habit was done*, weak evidence about energy/stress/focus/mood.

**The LOCA mismatch (the D4 root, restated empirically):** LOCA's *most‑weighted available* signal for inner state is the **weakest** one (habit checkboxes), while its **highest‑value** signals are either **under‑used** (explicit notes → fed only to a 7‑word lexicon; sleep *regularity* → not computed) or **ungranted** (Health/Calendar/Location for most users). And when the high‑value signals are absent, F1 renders their **absence as a low value** instead of as **"unknown."** Signal ranking + honest missing‑data handling is the empirical fix.

## Part B — Book notes

### 1. *Designing Data‑Intensive Applications* — Martin Kleppmann *(sensor‑data architecture angle)*
Reliable data systems: **immutable event logs** as source of truth, **derived views** you can recompute, schema evolution, and robust handling of streaming/late/duplicate data. Sensor pipelines live or die on this discipline.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** `SignalEvent`/`LogEntry` are the immutable sensor event log; states/patterns are derived views (already recomputed idempotently — verified). Carry **provenance and quality** from raw samples into every derived view; handle late/missing/duplicate sensor data explicitly (LOCA already dedups — good).
- **Anti‑patterns:** mutating raw samples; derived views that can't trace to source samples; ignoring late/missing data.
- **Design implications:** immutable sample log + quality/provenance metadata; recomputable views; explicit missing‑data semantics.
- **Open research questions:** does LOCA's pipeline preserve per‑sample quality/provenance to the UI?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** attach a quality/coverage tag to each `SignalEvent` and propagate it to readings.

### 2. *Deep Medicine* — Eric Topol
AI + **deep phenotyping** can personalize medicine and (paradoxically) restore the human element — *but* accuracy, validation, and humility are prerequisites; hype outruns evidence.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** the deep‑phenotyping vision (many signals → a rich personal model) is real and inspiring, but Topol's caution is the operative lesson: **validate, stay humble, augment the human.** LOCA's confident, unvalidated inner‑state claims (F1) are exactly what Topol warns against.
- **Anti‑patterns:** unvalidated confident health‑ish claims; hype over evidence; replacing the user's self‑knowledge.
- **Design implications:** validate signals before claiming; humble, augmenting framing; the user interprets.
- **Open research questions:** which LOCA signals are validated enough to speak on?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** a per‑signal "validation status" gating what LOCA will claim from it.

### 3. *The Patient Will See You Now* — Eric Topol
Democratized, **patient‑owned** medicine; the smartphone as a personal medical/sensing hub; **individualized (n=1)** care; the person owns and controls their data.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** LOCA's **local‑first, user‑owned** data model is the consumer embodiment of this vision — a genuine strategic and ethical asset (R4/R7). Lean into n=1 individualization (personal baselines) and user data ownership.
- **Anti‑patterns:** taking data ownership from the user; population models over n=1; cloud lock‑in of personal data.
- **Design implications:** user‑owned, on‑device, individualized data; export/delete rights.
- **Open research questions:** how far can honest n=1 modeling go on phone/watch data alone?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** foreground "your data, your device, your model" as a product stance.

### 4. *The Creative Destruction of Medicine* — Eric Topol
Each person is their **own unique dataset** ("the GIS of a human"); digitizing/individualizing health means modeling the individual against **their own** baseline, not a population.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** the **personal‑baseline** imperative again (Oura, R1; time‑series, R4): compare the user to themselves, never to a population constant. LOCA's placeholder baseline (F6) is precisely the population‑over‑individual error Topol destroys.
- **Anti‑patterns:** population baselines mislabeled as personal (F6); one‑size‑fits‑all norms.
- **Design implications:** per‑person adaptive baselines; disclose when a population prior is temporarily used.
- **Open research questions:** how much personal history before an individual baseline beats the population prior?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** replace F6 constants with per‑user baselines + an honest "learning your normal" state.

### 5. *Digital Phenotyping & Mobile Sensing* — reference texts/reviews (Onnela, Torous; StudentLife; JMIR/npj Digital Medicine reviews)
The academic field LOCA is actually operating in: passive smartphone/wearable data → behavioral/mental‑health markers. **Consensus findings:** prediction is **possible but modest**; effects are **highly individual**; **missing data and reproducibility** are major challenges; and **clinical validation lags** consumer claims.
- **Lessons for LOCA:** this literature is LOCA's reality check. It says: expect **modest, individual, noisy** signal; **handle missing data as first‑class** (not as low value — F1); build **per‑person** models; and **don't overclaim** (D3/D4/R7). LOCA is attempting real digital phenotyping — it should inherit the field's humility, not exceed its evidence.
- **Anti‑patterns:** claiming precision the field doesn't support; treating missing data as signal (F1); population models; ignoring reproducibility.
- **Design implications:** modest hedged claims; explicit missingness; per‑person calibration; validate before surfacing.
- **Open research questions:** which specific LOCA signal→state links replicate within a user over time?
- **Ideas to experiment with:** an internal replication check — does a claimed personal pattern hold across held‑out weeks?

## Part C — Research topics

### Group 1 — Sensing paradigms & platforms

**T1. Digital phenotyping.** Moment‑to‑moment, individual‑level quantification of behavior/state from personal devices (active + passive).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* LOCA *is* a digital‑phenotyping app; adopt the field's humility, per‑person modeling, and missing‑data rigor. → **D4**.
- *Anti‑patterns:* overclaiming; population models; missingness‑as‑signal.
- *Design implications:* modest, per‑person, missing‑aware inference.
- *Open questions:* which markers replicate for LOCA users?
- *Experiment:* held‑out replication of personal markers.

**T2. Mobile sensing.** Phone sensors → behavior; the practical toolkit.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* use phone sensors as *supporting* context, not primary emotional truth.
- *Anti‑patterns:* over‑reading phone sensors.
- *Design implications:* sensors as context, not verdict.
- *Open questions:* which phone sensors add honest value?
- *Experiment:* ablation — which sensors improve calibration.

**T3. Passive sensing.** Background collection with no user effort; low friction but low interpretability.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* passive data is convenient but ambiguous; pair with explicit input (check‑ins) for meaning.
- *Anti‑patterns:* interpreting passive data without explicit anchors.
- *Design implications:* passive + explicit fusion.
- *Open questions:* passive/explicit ratio for best accuracy?
- *Experiment:* passive‑only vs passive+check‑in calibration.

**T4. Context‑aware computing.** Systems adapting to situational context (Dey); context = who/what/where/when.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* use context to *frame* (time of day, place) honestly, not to assert inner state.
- *Anti‑patterns:* context → confident state claims.
- *Design implications:* context as framing/priors, disclosed.
- *Open questions:* which context most improves honest framing?
- *Experiment:* context‑framed vs context‑free readings.

**T5. Context inference.** Deriving situation (commuting, working, socializing) from sensors.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* inferred context is itself uncertain — hedge it and let the user correct.
- *Anti‑patterns:* confident context labels.
- *Design implications:* hedged, correctable context.
- *Open questions:* context‑inference accuracy on real data?
- *Experiment:* user‑confirmed context labeling.

**T6. HealthKit architecture.** On‑device typed sample store; **mandatory source/provenance** per sample; per‑type permission; app is one of many writers/readers.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* mirror HealthKit's discipline — typed, sourced, on‑device samples; respect per‑type consent (LOCA has `SignalSourceConsent`). HealthKit itself models the provenance discipline (R7). → **D2**.
- *Anti‑patterns:* dropping source on ingested samples; ignoring per‑type consent.
- *Design implications:* preserve HealthKit source metadata into LOCA's provenance.
- *Open questions:* is HealthKit source retained through LOCA's pipeline?
- *Experiment:* surface HealthKit source in provenance badges.

**T7. Apple ResearchKit.** Framework for research studies — consent flows, surveys, active tasks.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* borrow ResearchKit's **rigorous consent** patterns for data collection (R7 consent).
- *Anti‑patterns:* weak/implicit consent.
- *Design implications:* explicit, granular, revocable consent (LOCA has the ledger — surface it).
- *Open questions:* is LOCA's consent as clear as ResearchKit's?
- *Experiment:* a ResearchKit‑grade consent surface.

**T8. CareKit.** Framework for care plans/patient‑facing tracking.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* relevant only if LOCA moves toward care plans; otherwise a pattern reference for tracking UIs.
- *Anti‑patterns:* medicalizing a wellbeing app (R7‑T28).
- *Design implications:* stay non‑clinical.
- *Open questions:* none pressing.
- *Experiment:* n/a.

**T9. Wearable computing / T10. Smartwatch sensing.** Body‑worn continuous sensing; Apple Watch — HR, HRV, ECG, SpO2, activity, sleep, respiratory.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* the watch is the highest‑quality passive source; when granted, it materially improves signal (and reduces F1 deflation). But most users won't have one — design for its **absence** as the default.
- *Anti‑patterns:* assuming a watch; degrading dishonestly without one (F1).
- *Design implications:* graceful, honest behavior with and without a wearable.
- *Open questions:* how much does a wearable improve LOCA's calibration?
- *Experiment:* wearable vs phone‑only calibration comparison.

### Group 2 — Physiological signals

**T11. Heart rate.** Basic cardiac signal; highly context‑dependent (activity, caffeine, emotion).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* HR alone is ambiguous; use only with context and a personal baseline.
- *Anti‑patterns:* HR → emotion claims.
- *Design implications:* HR as context, baseline‑relative.
- *Open questions:* HR's marginal value for LOCA states?
- *Experiment:* HR ablation.

**T12. Resting heart rate.** Trends are a meaningful recovery/fitness marker; single readings less so.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* use RHR *trends vs personal baseline*, not absolute values.
- *Anti‑patterns:* absolute RHR judgments.
- *Design implications:* RHR trend, baseline‑relative.
- *Open questions:* RHR trend value for LOCA?
- *Experiment:* RHR‑trend feature test.

**T13. Heart rate variability (HRV).** Autonomic/stress‑recovery proxy; **extremely noisy day‑to‑day**; meaningless without a personal baseline and multi‑day smoothing.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* HRV is medium‑signal *only* baseline‑relative and smoothed; a single day is noise (Noise, R7). Never render raw daily HRV as a state.
- *Anti‑patterns:* single‑day HRV claims; population HRV norms.
- *Design implications:* smoothed, baseline‑relative HRV only.
- *Open questions:* smoothing window for honest HRV?
- *Experiment:* smoothed vs raw HRV in inference.

**T14. Respiratory rate.** Sleep‑derived; illness/stress signal; slow‑moving.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* a supporting illness/stress context signal, baseline‑relative.
- *Anti‑patterns:* over‑reading short‑term changes.
- *Design implications:* baseline‑relative, supporting role.
- *Open questions:* marginal value?
- *Experiment:* include/exclude in stress model.

**T15. Sleep stage research.** Stages (deep/REM/light/awake); consumer devices **approximate** via motion+HR and are **inaccurate vs polysomnography** — stage estimates are noisy.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* trust sleep **duration/timing/regularity** more than **stage** estimates; don't build confident claims on noisy stage data.
- *Anti‑patterns:* confident stage‑based claims; ignoring device inaccuracy.
- *Design implications:* prefer duration/regularity; hedge stages.
- *Open questions:* which sleep metric is most predictive for LOCA users?
- *Experiment:* regularity vs stage as an energy/mood predictor.

**T16. Circadian rhythm / T17. Chronobiology.** ~24h light‑entrained cycles drive energy/mood/cognition; **chronotypes** differ; misalignment harms wellbeing.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* LOCA already uses circadian priors in the energy model (verified). Personalize the circadian baseline (it's meant to learn) and respect chronotype — but keep it a **prior**, not a measurement (the C1.1 discipline LOCA already asserts).
- *Anti‑patterns:* population circadian curve as truth; ignoring chronotype.
- *Design implications:* personalized circadian baseline; prior not measurement.
- *Open questions:* how fast can a personal circadian curve be learned?
- *Experiment:* learned vs default circadian baseline.

### Group 4 — Biomarkers, fusion, quality

**T28. Digital biomarkers / T29. Behavioral biomarkers.** Objective device‑derived indicators (physiological or behavioral) used as health/state markers — **require analytical + clinical validation**; most consumer signals are **not** validated.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* treat LOCA's signals as **unvalidated behavioral indicators**, not biomarkers; do not imply clinical/biomarker status (R7‑T28). Claim modestly.
- *Anti‑patterns:* calling unvalidated signals "biomarkers"; clinical framing.
- *Design implications:* "indicator," never "biomarker"; non‑clinical language.
- *Open questions:* could any LOCA signal be validated as a personal biomarker?
- *Experiment:* per‑user analytical validation of one indicator.

**T30. Sensor fusion.** Combining sensors for robust inference; must handle **disagreement** (conflicting sources).
- *Lessons for LOCA:* fuse sources with proper weighting and **conflict handling** (LOCA has `SensorConflict` — surface it, R7). Fusion is where F1's renormalization must be correct.
- *Anti‑patterns:* naive weighted sums that don't renormalize (F1); ignoring conflicts.
- *Design implications:* principled fusion (Bayesian, R4) with conflict surfacing.
- *Open questions:* best fusion method for LOCA's heterogeneous sources?
- *Experiment:* Bayesian fusion vs current weighted sum (fixes F1).

**T31. Missing‑data handling.** The central sensor problem: **missing ≠ zero, missing ≠ low.** Options: honest absence, imputation (with uncertainty), or model‑level marginalization. Naive treatment biases everything.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* **this is the F1 bug named exactly.** LOCA treats missing sources as contributing low/nothing without renormalizing → systematic deflation. Fix: **renormalize over present sources** and mark absent dimensions **"unknown"** (LOCA already has an `isAbsent` concept — extend it end‑to‑end). → **D4**.
- *Anti‑patterns:* missing‑as‑zero/low (F1); silent imputation presented as measurement.
- *Design implications:* explicit missingness; renormalization; "unknown" over "low."
- *Open questions:* impute‑with‑uncertainty vs abstain — which is more honest for LOCA?
- *Experiment:* renormalized inference vs current (the F1 fix), calibration compared.

**T32. Sensor reliability.** Consumer sensors vary; motion artifacts, wear gaps, device differences degrade quality.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* weight sources by **reliability**; a flaky/low‑wear source should contribute less and lower confidence.
- *Anti‑patterns:* treating all sources as equally reliable.
- *Design implications:* reliability‑weighted fusion; confidence reflects source quality.
- *Open questions:* per‑source reliability estimates for LOCA?
- *Experiment:* reliability‑weighted inference.

**T33. Data quality assessment.** Validity/completeness/consistency; gate inference on quality; garbage in, confident garbage out.
- *Lessons for LOCA:* assess quality before inferring; **low quality → low confidence or abstain** (R7 selective prediction). This connects data quality directly to the Trust Contract.
- *Anti‑patterns:* inferring confidently from low‑quality data (F1 again).
- *Design implications:* a quality gate feeding the confidence/abstention decision.
- *Open questions:* what quality threshold gates a claim?
- *Experiment:* quality‑gated readings.

## Part D — R8 synthesis: LOCA's honest data strategy

### The answer: which data predicts outcomes vs noise
**High‑value, honest signals** (collect, weight, lead with): sleep **regularity/timing**, **physical activity/workouts**, **social interaction**, **mobility variety**, **explicit notes/check‑ins**, **light exposure**. **Medium (baseline‑relative, smoothed):** resting HR/HRV, calendar density, respiratory rate. **Low/noise (down‑weight or drop):** step‑count‑as‑mood, screen‑time‑as‑focus, single‑day HRV, weather, and **habit‑checkbox‑as‑emotional‑state** — LOCA's currently most‑weighted inner‑state input.

### The three data corrections LOCA needs (all D4)
1. **Fix missing‑data handling (F1).** Missing ≠ low. Renormalize over present sources; mark absent dimensions **"unknown."** This is the single highest‑leverage data fix and the empirical face of the F1 bug.
2. **Re‑weight toward high‑signal data.** Elevate explicit notes (beyond a 7‑word lexicon), compute **sleep regularity** and **mobility variety**, and **down‑weight** screen‑time/steps as emotional proxies. Stop leaning the inner‑state model on habit checkboxes.
3. **Personal baselines, not constants (F6).** Within‑person, adaptive baselines (Topol/Oura); disclose "learning your normal."

### Recurring data principles
- **Rank signal honestly**; weight by predictive value *and* reliability.
- **Missing is unknown, not low** — the cardinal data rule (fixes F1).
- **Within‑person n=1 > population norms** (Topol).
- **Passive is convenient but ambiguous** — anchor with explicit input.
- **Confidence must reflect per‑metric reliability** (steps ≠ sleep‑stage ≠ HRV).
- **Minimize collection**; drop low‑signal/high‑privacy‑cost sources.
- **On‑device, consented, governable** — privacy is a trust asset (R7).
- **Non‑clinical, unvalidated** — claim modestly (Topol/digital phenotyping).

### Anti‑patterns (data, additive to R2–R7)
- Missing‑data‑as‑low‑value (F1) — the core sensor error.
- Population baselines mislabeled personal (F6).
- Over‑weighting weak signals (checkboxes, screen time) for inner state.
- Uniform confidence across unequally‑reliable metrics.
- Collect‑everything (privacy cost + QS burnout).
- Clinical/biomarker framing of unvalidated signals.
- Ignoring sensor conflict/quality; naive non‑renormalized fusion (F1).
- Off‑device personal data; battery‑draining sensing.

### Design implications for LOCA V3 (the data program)
1. **Renormalize fusion + honest missingness** ("unknown" not "low"). *(the F1 fix — highest leverage)*
2. **Compute the high‑value features LOCA lacks:** sleep regularity, mobility variety; upgrade note handling beyond the 7‑word lexicon. *(re‑weight to signal)*
3. **Per‑person adaptive baselines** replacing F6 constants.
4. **Per‑metric reliability → per‑metric confidence**; quality‑gate claims (feeds the Trust Contract, R7).
5. **Surface consent + provenance + governance** (the ledger, export/delete) as user‑visible controls.
6. **Data minimization pass** — drop or down‑weight low‑value/high‑cost sources.
7. **Foreground on‑device privacy**; keep sensing battery‑efficient.
8. **Non‑clinical framing** everywhere signals are surfaced.

### How R8 changes LOCA's direction
R8 supplies the **empirical proof** behind D4: the digital‑phenotyping literature says LOCA's inner‑state modeling can only be **modest, individual, noisy, and missing‑data‑bound** — so the honest path is to **rank signal, weight to the strong sources, treat missing as unknown, and personalize baselines.** LOCA's architecture (multi‑source, consented, provenance‑aware) is *right*; its data *handling* (missing‑as‑low, checkbox‑over‑signal, population baseline) is the fixable core of D4. The strongest, most honest, most private signal LOCA has is the **user's own longitudinal record + explicit input** — which is exactly the asset R1–R7 already told it to lead with.
