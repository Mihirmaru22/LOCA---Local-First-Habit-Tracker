# LOCA — The Product Experience

*A design of what the user actually experiences. Not backend, not architecture, not
SwiftUI, not wireframes. The vision is frozen and assumed correct. This document only
answers the question the Apple HI team would ask: **"What does the user actually
experience?"***

---

## The whole product, in one paragraph

LOCA has almost no interface. It has a **present** you arrive at, a single **reach**
gesture that moves how far into your own life you are looking, the ability to **ask**
one thing at a time, and **views** — composed scenes of your real life that you read and
conclude from yourself. There are no tabs, no cards, no dashboard, no feed, no chat, no
charts. You point, you look, you follow what catches you, you set it down. That is the
entire product.

---

## 1. The first 10 minutes

No account wall. No feature carousel. No permission blast. No "Welcome to LOCA."

**0:00 — Open.** The screen is nearly empty and unhurried. It is the color of the light
you're actually in — dim if it's night, warm if it's evening. One quiet line, low on the
screen, addresses you as a person, not a user: not *"Get started,"* but something closer
to *"It's Tuesday evening. Let's begin where you are."* Nothing blinks. Nothing sells.
The first emotion is **calm, and slight surprise that an app is being this quiet.**

**0:30 — It asks one thing.** One. In your own words: *"Where are you in your life right
now?"* A single open line. You can type "starting my internship," "new dad," "burned
out," "coasting," "figuring it out," or nothing at all. There is a small *"skip"* — no
guilt attached. Most people answer, because it is disarmingly human and costs nothing.
This is not a form field. It is the only question the app will demand of you today.

**1:00 — It receives it with weight.** You type "starting my internship." The words don't
vanish into a database with a checkmark. They settle onto the screen and *stay* — named,
held, treated as the opening line of something. A beat of quiet. The feeling is **being
heard, not onboarded.**

**1:30 — Your first view composes.** Not a chart. Not stats. The present, framed as the
first page of this chapter: today, where you are, this beginning — rendered as one calm
scene you look *into*. It says, in effect, *this is day one of this chapter; from here I
can help you see how it actually goes.* You have created nothing, filled in nothing, and
yet something already exists. The feeling: **oh — this is looking at me, now. Not at a
dashboard.**

**3:00 — The honest ask.** Only now does it ask for anything technical, and it asks for
the *minimum*, framed by what it unlocks, never as "grant permissions": *"To notice
without asking you much, may I see your sleep and movement? I'll see more of your life,
and bother you less."* Each toggle explains the **view it makes possible**, in a sentence.
Declining every one is fine, stated plainly — *"That's okay. I'll work with less and see
less."* No dark pattern, no red, no nag. Whatever you allow, the tone is identical.

**5:00 — One true small thing.** With whatever you granted, it does exactly one honest
thing rather than ten fake ones. If you gave it Health: *"You've been on your feet more
than usual today"* — true, specific, unremarkable, and therefore trustworthy. If you gave
it nothing: it simply reflects the present with a precision that still makes you feel
*seen*. **One real observation beats a wall of manufactured insight,** and the app knows
it.

**7:00 — You reach back.** It shows you the one gesture that matters: you pull the view
back with your thumb, and time opens up behind the present. Today, this week — and then
almost nothing, because there is almost nothing yet. It does **not** fake a rich past. It
says: *"This is the first mark. It gets deeper every week."* The emptiness is framed as a
**beginning, not a failure.** You are watching the first stroke on a canvas, and it's
honest about that.

**9:00 — It lets you go.** No "complete your profile." No streak started. No
notification-permission nag disguised as a feature. No next step. You set it down having
felt seen exactly once and understanding precisely what it is for.

**The arc of the ten minutes:** quiet → noticed → heard → shown one true thing →
released. No dopamine. No checklist. The last emotion is **unhurried curiosity**, and the
knowledge that this thing will be worth returning to.

## 2. The present — what you see every time

Every time you open LOCA you arrive at the same place: **the present.** Not a home screen
of controls, not a summary of your numbers — a vantage on *you, now, here in your life.*

- **What stays stable:** the shape of it. You always arrive at the present. You always
  have exactly two moves available — **reach back** (into your life) or **ask** (a
  question). Nothing relocates. It is a place, and places don't rearrange themselves.
- **What's alive:** the *content* is never the same twice, because your life isn't. The
  light matches the hour. The texture of the last few days is the near ground under the
  present. The current chapter is quietly named. It feels alive not because things
  animate, but because it is **truthfully different every day** — the aliveness is real,
  not decorative.
- **What draws attention:** at most **one** soft thread — a single thing worth looking
  into right now, gently lit, never demanding. Some days there is none, and that is a
  valid, calm state, not an empty one. There is never a wall of things wanting you.
- **How it refuses to be a dashboard:** the one-view rule. There is never more than one
  thing to read at a time. No tiles. No metrics grid. No "at a glance" everything. If you
  want more, you *reach* or you *ask* — you are never handed a panel of your whole life to
  scan. Scanning is what dashboards are for, and there is nothing here to scan.

Two moves from the present. That is the whole surface. The minimalism *is* the design.

## 3. The daily rhythm

Same instrument, different lean, by the hour. It never announces this; you just find it
matches the time of day.

- **Morning** — the present leans slightly *forward*. If you look, you can see how you
  tend to be on mornings like this — the shape of the day ahead in the light of your own
  patterns. **Never a to-do list, never a plan, never a coach's agenda.** An orientation,
  not an instruction. You may glance and go.
- **Afternoon** — mostly silent. A life is in progress; the instrument does not
  interrupt it. Open it and it's simply the neutral present. It has the confidence to
  have nothing to say.
- **Evening** — the present leans slightly *back*. The day just lived settles into the
  near ground; if you look, you can watch today take its place in the week. This is the
  natural moment to **focus** anything the day left soft (learning happens here, only if
  you choose it, and only because *you* want the view clear).
- **Late night** — the gentlest state, dim and low-demand. It will **never** surface
  anything heavy, evaluative, or anxious after dark — no "you missed your goals," no hard
  truth unasked-for, no red anything. At most it offers a single soft moment of the day
  worth keeping. Self-reflection must never become self-criticism at a vulnerable hour;
  the app's restraint at midnight is a designed feature, not an absence of one.

**What should never appear, at any hour:** notifications that nag, streaks, badges,
red or negative framing, "falling behind," any comparison to other people, a feed of
anything, unsolicited advice, or a verdict about your life.

## 4. Perspective — the interaction, concretely

You ask: *"Am I happier since starting my internship?"*

**What appears — not an answer.** LOCA does **not** reply *"Yes, 12% happier."* A reply
would make it a chatbot with a verdict, and it is neither. Instead it composes **a view**:
it finds the seam in your life where the internship began and sets the stretch *before*
and the stretch *after* beside each other — not as two bar charts, but as two spans of
the same continuous ground you already know how to read, the seam marked between them. The
parts it is unsure of are **soft** — visibly hazy — so you always know what it knows and
what it's guessing. You look at the two stretches and **you** draw the conclusion. It will
not draw it for you.

**How you move through it.** Woven into the view are a few **soft-lit differences** — the
things that actually changed across the seam: *you're around different people now*, *you
sleep less but your mornings read brighter*, *your weekends inverted*. These aren't a list
to read; they're **threads to pull.** Touch "brighter mornings" and it becomes the next
view — your mornings, before and after the seam, composed the same way. Touch "different
people" and a view of who you're around now composes. **One question becomes a walk.**

**How you go deeper.** Every view you land on has its own soft threads. "Brighter
mornings" → pull → *is it the new commute?* → pull → the commute across the seam → pull →
the specific stretch of it that reads best. You descend by curiosity, one clear view per
step, never a branching tree to manage — the view you're in *is* the menu for the next
one.

**The gestures — the entire vocabulary:**
- **Ask** — aim a question (the only text you ever type).
- **Reach** — pull the view back or lean it in to change how far across time you're
  looking.
- **Pull a thread** — touch a soft element to compose the adjacent view.
- **Focus** — sharpen a hazy part by confirming or correcting it (and in doing so, teach
  it — because *you* wanted the view clear).
- **Set down** — look away. You leave by lowering the instrument, not by closing a flow.

**How you leave.** There is nothing to finish, save, or dismiss. You looked; now you go
back to living. No artifact was created, no session completed, no "did you get value?"
prompt. You set it down and the present is there again the next time. **Leaving is
frictionless because nothing was ever a task.**

## 5. Navigation — moving through years without charts

There is **one axis**: how far into your life you are looking. You move along it with a
single continuous gesture — pull back, lean in. It is not a set of tabs (day / week /
month / year); it is a **continuous change of resolution**, and the resolution is *felt
granularity*, not zoom level:

- **Near** — today and this week are vivid and grainy; you can almost feel the individual
  moments.
- **Pull back** — days lose their separate identity but their **texture** remains: a
  stretch where your energy ran high, a patch that was heavy, a bright week.
- **Further** — seasons become regions with their own temperature and weather; the shape
  of a whole year is something you can take in at once — the thing no human can hold in
  their head.
- **All the way** — chapters as distinct territories, and the turning points as the seams
  between them.

The transition is **smooth and continuous** — you are not switching modes, you are pulling
one lens back and watching your life's resolution dissolve from moments into shape. That
continuity is the entire reason it doesn't feel like charts: charts have discrete modes;
this has one motion.

**And you navigate by meaning, not by date.** As you pull back, the turning points stay
fixed as **landmarks** — *the move*, *the internship*, *the hard winter*. You can lean
toward a landmark to bring its chapter near, the way you'd navigate by memory rather than
by calendar. You never scrub a date slider. You travel your own life by the things that
mattered in it.

## 6. Exploration — curiosity, not productivity

There is no "Explore" section, because exploration isn't a place — it's what naturally
happens. Every view carries a few soft threads (uncertain parts to focus, adjacent
questions to pull). **Following a thread composes the next view.** Curiosity becomes a
walk through your own life, question to question, each step a single clear view.

Three rules keep this curiosity and not consumption:
- **The app never pulls the thread for you.** No autoplay, no "up next," no feed pushing
  the next thing. It only *offers*; you *choose*. The walk is yours to take or stop.
- **The current view is the only menu.** There is no separate list of things you could
  explore — what you're looking at suggests what to look at next, and nothing else does.
  This is why there is no navigation tree to get lost in.
- **Dead ends are honest.** When a thread leads somewhere the data can't yet support, it
  says so — the view is mostly soft, and it tells you it will sharpen with time. Curiosity
  is never punished with a fabricated answer.

One perspective leads to another because **every answer is made of smaller questions**,
and each is touchable.

## 7. The empty state, and the full one

**Brand new — no habits, no Health, no data.** The honest problem: there is nothing to
look at yet, and faking depth would poison the trust the whole product runs on. So day one
does not fake it. Its magic is small and real:

- It reflects the **present** with a clarity that makes you feel noticed rather than
  processed.
- It lets you **name where you are** (§1) and treats that naming with weight — you have a
  named beginning, which is more than most apps give you after a month.
- It makes a **visible promise**: the reach gesture shows you an almost-empty life *and*
  shows you it filling — you leave understanding exactly what will accrue and why it's
  worth returning. The emptiness is a first mark, framed as the start of something, not a
  barren dashboard.

Day one gives you **one moment of feeling seen and an honest beginning.** That is enough,
and it is more truthful than any onboarding that pretends to know you.

**Ten years in.** The present now sits on deep, textured ground. Pull back and there is a
*life* down there with a real shape. Your questions get answers with weight — *"happier
than five years ago?"* is now answerable, and you can *see* it. The present occasionally
**turns toward you** on its own — rarely, and only at a genuine fork — because it now has
enough of your history to say *"you've stood here before; the last two times, this is what
happened,"* and set your own past quietly beside your decision. The instrument has earned
authority it did not have on day one, and it wields it sparingly. What changed in ten
years is not the interface — it is the **depth of what you're looking at.** The eyepiece
is the same; the life behind it is vast.

## 8. Delight — moments, not animations

None of these are effects. They are moments that make a person go still:

- **"I forgot that."** The first time it returns a real moment from a year ago at exactly
  the moment it's relevant — a small piece of your own life you had genuinely lost.
- **Seeing you made it out.** Pulling back over a hard stretch you lived through and
  *seeing its shape from outside* — realizing, with your eyes, that you came through it.
  Perspective you literally could not have had while inside it.
- **It listened.** The moment it's about to be wrong about you, you focus it, and it
  *takes* the correction — the feeling of an instrument that adjusts to you rather than
  insisting.
- **Accompanied, not advised.** At a real decision, it sets a relevant piece of your own
  past beside you and says nothing else. Not "you should." Just *"here's what you've done
  before."* The feeling of not being alone in the choice, without being told what to do.
- **Two chapters rhyme.** Discovering that two eras you thought were unrelated have the
  same shape — a pattern in your life you'd never have noticed from inside any single part
  of it.

These are **perspective-delights**, not dopamine-delights — rare, quiet, and earned. The
product would rather give you five of these a year than a hundred cheap ones.

## 9. What was deleted, and why

Everything was challenged. Here is what did not survive first principles:

- **The home dashboard — deleted.** Replaced by *the present*: a vantage you arrive at,
  not a screen of controls and tiles.
- **Tabs — deleted.** There are no parallel sections to switch between. One instrument,
  one time-axis, one action. Tabs imply modes; there are none.
- **Cards — deleted.** No tiles, ever. One view at a time. A card wall is a dashboard
  wearing a costume.
- **The timeline chart — deleted.** Replaced by the continuous reach, where you feel
  granularity change rather than read a plotted axis.
- **The feed — deleted.** No stream, no autoplay, no infinite scroll, no "up next."
  Exploration is threads *you* pull.
- **Chat — deleted.** You may *ask*, but the reply is a **view**, not a message. There is
  no conversation history, no chat bubbles, no back-and-forth. Asking is aiming, not
  talking.
- **Verdicts — deleted.** It never concludes for you. It composes what you need to see and
  leaves the conclusion where it belongs — with you.

**What remains** is startlingly small: a present you arrive at, a reach that moves you
through your life, the ability to ask one thing, views you read yourself, and threads you
follow. An eyepiece and a focus ring pointed at your own life. **The reduction is the
product.**

## 10. The Apple test — brutally honest

Would Jobs, Ive, Kay, Victor, and the iPhone team call this *"a genuinely new way to
interact with personal information"* — or *"another app with a better philosophy"*?

**What is genuinely new, and would survive the room:**
- **The reach.** A single continuous axis where you navigate your own life by *felt
  granularity* and by *meaning-landmarks*, not by discrete day/week/month/year modes and a
  date slider. No personal-data app works this way. **Ive would respect the restraint;
  Jobs would respect the ruthlessness of §9's deletions.** This is real.
- **The no-verdict view.** Composing a scene you conclude from, rather than delivering an
  answer, is a different *act* than any tracker or assistant performs. If it works, it is
  new.
- **The surface reduction.** An app with no tabs, cards, feed, dashboard, or chat — just
  an eyepiece — is a category-level act of subtraction most teams cannot bring themselves
  to make.

**Where the room would push hard — and where I won't pretend:**
- **Kay's question:** *"Is the 'view' a genuinely new representation, or a chart with
  softer edges?"* This is the crux, and it is **not yet proven.** If the composed view is
  secretly a chart, the whole thing collapses into prettier analytics and the answer
  becomes "better philosophy, same app." Everything rides on one real view feeling like
  *seeing* rather than *reading*.
- **Victor's push, and it's the sharpest:** what's designed here is a beautiful
  **read-only** instrument — you look *through* it. He would want to *think with* it: grab
  a thread and **bend** it — *"what if I'd kept the old commute?"* — and watch the view
  respond. Looking is receptive; the frontier is **manipulable** — a place you reason
  inside, not just a scene you observe. The current design stops one step short of that,
  and that step is where "new interaction" becomes "new medium."

**The honest verdict:** This is **on the threshold** of genuinely new, not safely past it.
The navigation and the subtraction *are* new. The view is *potentially* new and currently
unproven. It is not yet "another app with a better philosophy" — the interaction grammar
is too reduced and too unlike the category for that — but it earns the stronger sentence
only if two things are proven with real hands on real data:

1. **One composed view feels like seeing, not reading** (answers Kay).
2. **At least one view can be *bent*, not just looked at** (answers Victor).

Prove those two and the room says *"genuinely new."* Skip them and it's a gorgeous,
better-behaved analytics app. The vision is frozen and sound; **the newness is real but
still unproven, and it will be won or lost in a single hand-built view — not in another
document.**

---

## The one thing to build next

Not more of this document. **One view. By hand. For one real question, on one real
person's real months of data.** Put it in front of them and watch their face for the
difference between *"huh, a chart"* and *"…oh."* Then try to let them **bend** it. That
single artifact decides whether LOCA is a new way to see your life or a nicer way to read
about it — and no amount of writing can decide it in this document's place.
