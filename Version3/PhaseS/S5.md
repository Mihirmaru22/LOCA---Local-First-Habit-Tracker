# LOCA Version 3 · Phase S — Synthesis
## S5 — The Learning Philosophy (How LOCA Should Learn)

> **The single question S5 answers:** *How should LOCA learn — evolve its understanding of a human over months and years without losing trust?*
>
> This is a philosophy of **learning over time**, not machine learning. It contains **no implementation, no database design, no ML architecture, no features, no UI.** Every conclusion traces to Phase R (R1–R10), S1 (**P1–P12**), S2 (the strata **Record → Reflection → Understanding**, the intelligence **ceiling**), S3 (the **self‑understanding gap**), and S4 (the reasoning constitution — the evidence, confidence, and inference hierarchies).
>
> **The journey S5 governs:** a person starts at **zero data**. Years later LOCA may hold thousands of observations. S5 defines how *ignorance becomes understanding, uncertainty becomes confidence, and confidence stays humble* — the temporal extension of S4. Where S4 asked "given this evidence now, what may I believe?", S5 asks "as evidence accumulates over years, how should belief grow, harden, soften, decay, and be forgotten — without ever betraying the person's trust?"

---

## Part I — The Learning Philosophy (the stance)

LOCA learns as a **slow, humble, evidence‑earned, reversible accretion, taught primarily by the person, in which the record is permanent and every belief is provisional.** Six commitments:

1. **Learning is accretion, not revelation.** Understanding is built grain by grain from corroborated evidence, never asserted in a leap. One observation is a hypothesis; repetition makes a pattern; sustained repetition makes provisional knowledge. *(S4 Ev.4; R7 Noise — one instance is not a pattern.)*
2. **The person is the teacher.** The highest‑authority learning signal is the person's own input and correction; passive accretion is supporting material, never the master. *(S4 learning hierarchy; R4 human‑as‑corrector; P1, P5.)*
3. **The record is permanent; beliefs are provisional.** LOCA never forgets what happened (Stratum 1) and never treats what it *infers* (Stratum 2) as fixed. This single separation makes safe forgetting possible. *(P6, P12; R2 Schacter.)*
4. **Confidence tracks evidence across time — and decays.** Belief strengthens only with independent, repeated, recent, within‑person corroboration, and weakens as its evidence ages or the person changes. Confidence is never a ratchet that only rises. *(S4; R4 continual learning; R8 personal baselines.)*
5. **Learn in proportion to stability.** Volatile things (mood) are learned lightly and forgotten quickly; stable things (values, identity) are learned slowly, over years, and held most tentatively of all. *(R2 traits vs states; adaptive‑systems principle.)*
6. **Learn only in service of the person's understanding.** LOCA learns to help the person see themselves — never to profile, predict‑for‑engagement, or manipulate. What is learned, and why, is bounded by the mission. *(S3; P7.)*

**The governing sentence:** *LOCA earns its understanding of a person the way trust is earned — slowly, from evidence, revisably, and always subordinate to the person's own word.*

## Part II — What LOCA should and should not learn

**What LOCA should learn:** the person's patterns, tendencies, preferences, routines, relationships (as they matter to the person), health rhythms, skills, environments, and — slowly and only as the person authors it — their goals, values, and evolving identity. Always as *material for the person's understanding*, never as a fixed dossier. *(S3.)*

**What LOCA should never learn (the boundary):**
- Anything to **manipulate, predict‑for‑engagement, or profile the person for another's ends.** *(P7; R3.)*
- Any **fixed verdict** on the person's worth, identity, or whether they are "doing well" — the ceiling is not a knowledge category. *(S2 ceiling; P1.)*
- Anything the person **has not consented to** the companion learning. *(P5.)*
- A **cage of the past self** — a stale model that holds the person to who they used to be. Learning must never become imprisonment. *(R4 stale user models.)*

**Temporary vs long‑term (the volatility axis):**
- **Temporary** (working/short‑term, decays fast): the current moment, this week's states, a mood, a transient context.
- **Long‑term** (consolidated, ages slowly): corroborated habits, preferences, routines, relationships, health baselines, stable tendencies.
- **Slowest / most stable** (years): values and identity — and even these evolve.

**Which beliefs auto‑expire vs require re‑confirmation vs are never forgotten:**
- **Auto‑expire/decay:** any inferred belief whose supporting evidence has aged past the volatility of its subject (a "state pattern" unseen for weeks weakens; a broken habit retires).
- **Require the person's confirmation:** anything about inner state, the *nature* of a relationship, goals, values, or identity — LOCA may hypothesize, but may not hold these as known without the person's word. *(S4 inference hierarchy.)*
- **Require repeated evidence before becoming knowledge:** every pattern and tendency (one event is never enough). *(Rule 1, 2.)*
- **Never forgotten:** the **record** — what actually happened — unless the person directs its deletion (sovereignty). *(P6, P5.)*

## Part III — Knowledge Lifecycles

Each kind of knowledge has a characteristic **volatility (half‑life)** that sets how much evidence it needs, how fast it decays, and how tentatively it's held. Ordered from most volatile to most stable.

| Knowledge | Discovered by | Confidence built by | Updated | Decays | Forgotten when | Challenged when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Facts (events)** | recorded/confirmed | certain at capture | only by correction of a wrong entry | never (permanent) | only on the person's deletion | only if the person says the record is wrong |
| **Emotional patterns** *(states/tendencies)* | repeated corroborated within‑person states across contexts | many observations, across contexts, over weeks | continuously, recency‑weighted | fast — a chapter change can shift them | the pattern stops recurring | always held as *tendency*, never trait; re‑examined each period |
| **Routines** *(temporal structure)* | repeated timing regularities | regularity over weeks | continuously | fast on disruption (transition) | disrupted routine retires to "past" | on any discontinuity |
| **Habits** *(actions)* | repeated logged behavior | frequency + consistency | continuously | fast when logging/behavior stops | a broken habit becomes a *past* habit (kept in record) | on context change |
| **Health patterns** | sensed data over time, baseline‑relative | personal baseline + repetition | continuously | baselines adapt; old ones age out | superseded by a new baseline | on a health change; framed non‑clinically |
| **Environments** *(places/contexts)* | recurring location/context | recurrence over time | on moves | past environments fade in salience | never from record; salience fades | on relocation |
| **Skills** | repeated activity + demonstrated progress | demonstrated repetition | continuously | unused skills fade *in belief* | retired to record when long unused | on prolonged inactivity |
| **Preferences** | repeated choices + explicit statement | repetition + statement | new choices | slowly (preferences drift) | when consistently contradicted over time | when behavior diverges from stated preference |
| **Relationships** *(who matters)* | recurring presence in real context + confirmation | recurrence + the person's confirmation | as presence changes | *salience* decays with absence | never from record; salience fades | on a life change; the *nature* of a relationship is confirmation‑only |
| **Goals** | explicit statement (never inferred as fact) | stated = known; never assumed | the person restates | goals complete or expire; stale goals need re‑confirmation | retired to "past goals," kept in record | when actions diverge — gently, as reflection, never judgment |
| **Values** | explicit, or inferred‑then‑confirmed | high only when the person confirms | rarely; person‑led | very slowly | never; they evolve | rarely, and only reflectively |
| **Identity** *(who they are/becoming)* | the person's own narration + very stable cross‑context patterns they confirm | held most tentatively of all — the person authors identity | slowly, by the person | evolves across life; old identities become "who I was" | never; part of the life story | never by the system — this is the person's domain (Stratum 3) |

**The unifying law:** *the more identity‑adjacent and consequential a kind of knowledge, the more the person must author or confirm it, the more evidence it demands, the more slowly it changes, and the more tentatively LOCA holds it.* Mood is learned lightly and forgotten quickly; identity is approached slowly, held humbly, and owned by the person.

## Part IV — The Memory Architecture (conceptual)

Seven conceptual memories, mapped to the strata and to human memory research (R2/R4). Each exists for a reason; information *consolidates* upward with corroboration and *decays* downward or fades without it — **except the episodic record, which never moves and never fades.**

1. **Working memory — the now.** The current moment's signals and context being reasoned over. *Exists to* compose the present read. *Holds* only what's live. *Disappears* after use — it is never stored as belief. *(S4 reasoning operates here.)*
2. **Short‑term understanding — recent days/weeks.** Provisional recent patterns. *Exists to* read the present against the near past. *Holds* tentative, recency‑weighted observations. *Consolidates* upward if corroborated; *decays* if not. *(R8 baselines form here.)*
3. **Long‑term understanding — semantic.** Consolidated, corroborated knowledge about the person (stable preferences, tendencies). *Exists to* carry durable understanding across time. *Holds* what has been repeatedly and independently corroborated. *Ages/decays* if the evidence stops. *(R4; R2 semantic memory.)*
4. **Episodic memory — the record.** Specific, timestamped events: Stratum 1, the truthful record. *Exists to* be the permanent, traceable ground of everything. *Holds* what actually happened. *Never* moves, fades, or is forgotten (save the person's deletion). All understanding is built from it and traceable to it. *(P6; R4 immutable log; R2 autobiographical memory.)*
5. **Semantic understanding — generalized knowledge.** The person's stable facts, preferences, values — abstracted from episodes. *Exists to* hold the "what is true of this person." *Consolidated* slowly from repeated episodes. *(R2.)*
6. **Procedural understanding — how the person operates.** Routines, habits, patterns‑of‑doing. *Exists to* hold the "how" of the person. *Built* from repeated behavior; *decays* on disruption. *(R3 habit/context.)*
7. **Identity‑level understanding — who they are.** The slowest, most tentatively held, most person‑authored. *Exists to* hold a *reflection* of identity — never a verdict. *Holds* only what is very stable, cross‑context, and the person confirms. *Adjacent to Stratum 3 — the system reflects it; the person owns it.* *(S2; R2 narrative identity.)*

**Movement (consolidation):** an episode that repeats and corroborates moves from episodic → short‑term → long‑term/semantic/procedural; only the most stable, cross‑context, person‑confirmed patterns approach identity‑level. **Decay:** a long‑term belief unsupported by recent evidence weakens back toward hypothesis and may retire. **The invariant:** *beliefs derived from the record move and fade; the record itself never does.* This is what lets LOCA adapt to who a person is becoming without ever erasing who they were.

## Part V — Belief & Confidence Evolution (the journey from zero to years)

**The arc of understanding, honestly paced:**
- **t₀ — zero data:** total, honest ignorance. Only disclosed priors (explicitly *not about you*). No confident interpretation exists, and LOCA says so. *(S3 honest empty state; S4 abstention.)*
- **Early — days to weeks:** facts accumulate (certain, Stratum 1); first hypotheses form (tentative). No confident interpretation yet; confidence is honestly low. *(S4 confidence L1/L4.)*
- **Mid — weeks to months:** patterns corroborate; some rise to probable observations; personal baselines form; confidence rises *only where earned* and stays humble. *(S4 L3; R8 baselines.)*
- **Long — months to years:** rich semantic/procedural understanding; stable patterns; an identity‑level *reflection* emerges from the person's narration. Confidence is high on stable things — but never certain, always correctable, always decaying if unsupported. *(P12.)*

**The confidence curve is not monotonic.** It rises with corroboration and *falls* with contradiction, staleness, and life‑change. A companion whose confidence only ever increases is miscalibrated by construction. *(R7 calibration; R4.)*

**Handling contradictory evidence over time:** weight by authority (person > sensor), recency, and independence; a single contrary instance is a data point, not a refutation (don't over‑fit to it *either*); genuine unresolved conflict *lowers* confidence or triggers abstention, and the conflict is recorded as evidence, never silently resolved. *(S4 belief update; R7 SensorConflict.)*

**How exceptional life events reshape prior beliefs:** routine change requires repetition to shift a belief — but a **confirmed significant event** (a loss, a move, a birth, an illness) may legitimately and *permanently* reset baselines, open a new chapter, and reshape beliefs. The asymmetry is resolved by authority: **the person confirms significance; the system never unilaterally decides a life has turned.** *(R3 habit discontinuity; R2 turning points; R1 chapters/events; P1, P5.)*

**Distinguishing a temporary mood from a long‑term trait** — the single most important learning distinction, because getting it wrong causes real harm (R2 Learned Optimism):
- A **mood** is a momentary deviation (working/short‑term memory) — learned lightly, forgotten fast, never generalized.
- A **pattern** is a repeated, corroborated, cross‑context regularity (long‑term) — held as a *tendency*, always with confidence and provenance.
- A **trait** — a fixed, characterological attribute — is **never asserted at all** (the ceiling). LOCA may say "energy has tended lower over these weeks"; it may never say "you are a low‑energy person." A bad week is never allowed to become an identity. *(S4 ceiling; R2; P8.)*

## Part VI — The Forgetting Philosophy

**Two kinds of forgetting, sharply separated:**
- **Forgetting a belief/interpretation** — *adaptive and necessary.* Stale beliefs must fade so the person is met as who they are now, not who they were. *(R4 stale models.)*
- **Forgetting the record** — *forbidden.* What happened is sacred and permanent. LOCA forgets *beliefs*, never *facts*. *(P6.)*

**Mechanisms of healthy forgetting:**
- **Active decay:** an inferred belief loses confidence as its supporting evidence ages, at a rate proportional to the volatility of its subject (a mood‑pattern decays in weeks; a value in years).
- **Retirement, not deletion:** a broken habit, a completed goal, an old routine, a former environment are *retired* to "past," not erased — the record keeps them; the *live* understanding drops them as current.
- **Salience fade:** a person seen less becomes less salient — without being forgotten from the record. *(R8 salience.)*

**The ethics of forgetting (the resolution):** a companion must neither *pretend the past didn't happen* nor *hold a person to a stale model of themselves.* Both are betrayals. The resolution is the invariant from Part IV: **the record remembers everything; the live understanding remembers what is still true.** History is preserved; beliefs are current. *(P6, P12; R2.)*

**User‑directed forgetting:** the person may direct deletion of the record itself — the one case where facts may be forgotten, exercised at the person's command, because sovereignty outranks even the sanctity of the record. *(P5.)*

## Part VII — Adaptation, Personalization & the Long‑Term User Model

**Adaptation philosophy:** LOCA grows *with* the person — baselines adapt, chapters turn, understanding tracks who they are becoming — rather than anchoring them to who they were. Adaptation is the applied form of "beliefs decay, the record persists." *(R8; R4.)*

**Personalization philosophy:** strictly **n=1** — built from the person's own data, never from population norms presented as personal. A prior borrowed from a population is disclosed as *not about you* and is retired the moment personal evidence exists. *(R8; R10 Topol; S4 Ev.7.)*

**Long‑term user model philosophy:** the model of the person is a **living reflection the person co‑authors** — not a fixed profile the system owns. It is:
- **Editable** — the person can inspect and correct what LOCA believes about them. *(P5, P12.)*
- **Ageing** — beliefs decay; stale ones weaken. *(R4.)*
- **Correctable and honest** — correction overrides inference and visibly updates the model. *(S4 belief update.)*
- **Inspectable with history** — the person can see what was believed, on what evidence, and why it changed. *(P4.)*
- **Never a cage** — it exists to help the person understand themselves today, not to fix them as their past self. *(the never‑learn boundary.)*

The long‑term model is the **compounding asset** of the whole product (R1/R10) — but it compounds in *value to the person*, not in the system's *certainty about them*. Over years, LOCA knows the person better and holds that knowledge *more humbly and more traceably*, not more absolutely.

## Part VIII — The Learning Rules (the commandments), each justified

1. **Never overfit to one event.** A single instance is noise until repeated; one data point is not a pattern. *(R7 Noise; S4 Ev.4.)*
2. **Strong claims require repeated, independent, within‑person, recent evidence.** Confidence is earned by corroboration, not by a vivid instance. *(S4; R8.)*
3. **Rare but *confirmed significant* events may permanently reshape the model.** The exception to Rule 1 — resolved by the person confirming significance, never the system deciding it. *(R3 discontinuity; R2 turning points; R1 chapters.)*
4. **Identity changes slowly, is authored by the person, and is held most tentatively of all.** The deepest knowledge is the person's to write, not the system's to assert. *(R2; S2 Stratum 3; P1.)*
5. **Match evidence‑needed and decay‑rate to volatility.** Learn volatile things lightly and forget them fast; learn stable things slowly and let them persist. *(adaptive systems; R8 baselines.)*
6. **Context always matters.** A state or behavior means little without its context; learning strips of context are misleading. *(R3 context‑cued behavior; R8.)*
7. **Refine beliefs; never overwrite history.** New evidence updates the interpretation while the record of what was believed — and what happened — is preserved. *(P6, P12; R2 Schacter.)*
8. **Distinguish mood from pattern from trait; never promote a mood to a trait.** The most harm‑preventing rule in the file. *(R2 Learned Optimism; S4 ceiling; P8.)*
9. **Decay stale beliefs; never hold a person to an outdated model of themselves.** Learning must not become a cage. *(R4 stale models.)*
10. **The person is the teacher; explicit input and correction outrank all accreted inference.** *(S4 learning hierarchy; R4; P1, P5.)*
11. **Learn only what serves the person's understanding.** Never learn to profile, predict‑for‑engagement, or manipulate. *(S3; P7.)*
12. **Preserve provenance through consolidation.** However abstracted a belief becomes, it must always trace back to the episodes that formed it. *(P4; R4 lineage.)*

---

## The learning philosophy, in one statement

> **LOCA learns the way trust is earned: slowly, from repeated evidence, taught above all by the person, holding every inference provisionally and every fact permanently. It builds understanding grain by grain — a hypothesis from one event, a tendency from many, knowledge only from the sustained and corroborated — and it matches how tightly it holds a belief to how stable the thing believed actually is: mood learned lightly and forgotten fast, identity approached slowly, held humbly, and left to the person to author. It forgets beliefs but never the record; it decays what is stale rather than caging the person in their past; it lets confirmed turning points reshape the model while refusing to overfit to a single day; and over years it comes to know the person better only by holding that knowledge more humbly and more traceably, never more absolutely. LOCA grows alongside a life without ever presuming to have finished learning it.**

This is the conceptual foundation for every future learning system in Version 3. Any learning process that ratchets confidence without decay, promotes a mood to a trait, overwrites the record, holds a person to a stale self, learns for the product rather than the person, or severs a belief from its provenance is **unconstitutional by construction.**
